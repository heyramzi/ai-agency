# Local SEO, where the click did not die

Read this when the target has a service area or a physical address. Local is the one segment where
the zero-click collapse in [measurement.md](measurement.md) does not apply yet, and the reason is
simple: an assistant can answer "how much does a water heater replacement cost" and cannot replace
the water heater. Somebody with a leak at 9pm still has to reach a person.

The consequence for planning is that the national informational playbook and the local playbook
diverge completely, and blending an agency's blended numbers hides it. Selling four blog posts a
month to a local business is the common failure: one measured client was paying over $8,000 a month
for SEO, holding 100,000 clicks a month from readers in the wrong cities, and getting almost no
local calls. Traffic that is not attached to a service area is not revenue.

## The Google Business Profile is the entity, not a listing

Almost every local surface resolves through it, Google itself, Gemini, the map pack, and the ads
if they are linked. ChatGPT is the exception and reads Bing Places instead, so claim both.

- **Fill every field, and claim every category the business legitimately qualifies for.** Not
  keyword stuffing; completeness.
- **Lock the profile.** A competitor can hit "suggest an edit" and, with enough Local Guide
  standing, have it accepted. A personal injury firm had its category changed to *airline*, and the
  calls stopped that day. Third-party tools lock against this.
- **Post regularly.** Posts rarely add views directly; they convert the views already arriving into
  calls, they feed the AI Overview link carousels, they earn a keyword-match badge in Maps, and
  going three months silent reads to Google as a business that may have closed.
- **Suspension causes, all avoidable:** a UPS box, a PO box, a coworking or shared address, several
  profiles at one address, a home office presented as visitable, or invented city listings. Multiple
  legitimate locations are fine; each needs its own real reviews, or thin profiles dilute the set.

## Reviews: the language matters more than the stars

Two thresholds and one signal.

- **Ten reviews** is roughly where a profile starts being shown with confidence.
- **Velocity** is what Google reads afterwards. A steady eight a month beats fifty in one month and
  silence after; the pattern reads as an active business.
- **The words inside the review are the AI signal.** Assistants match what somebody asks against
  what reviewers actually said, so a review naming the specific service and the outcome does work
  that a bare five stars cannot. Ask for the detail, not just the rating.

A 4.7-4.9 average converts better than a flat 5.0, which reads as filtered. Route ratings of three
and below to a human internally before they reach the public form, and answer them yourself:
an AI-written reply to a genuinely angry customer makes it worse. Automate replies to the good ones.

## The site architecture: one page per category, one per service, city in the title

The pattern that holds for local, and the one that AI search happens to reward for the same reason
Google does, because the entity becomes unmistakable:

- A page for **every category** on the Business Profile, and a page for **every service**, with the
  city in the title tag on all of them, mirroring the profile's own structure exactly.
- **Service x city, zipped together**: `emergency plumber <city>` for each real service area, or
  each municipality within one city. Keep these to dozens. They are near-duplicates by nature, and
  hundreds of them is a thin-content signal, not a scale strategy.
- Local blog posts carry the city too. `best wedding venues <city>` from a wedding photographer is
  the shape: it targets the buyer of the service without being about the service, and it converts
  because the reader is one step from needing you.
- Every blog post links to the money page; every money page links to the two or three best posts.

**Publish local prices and local specifics, because almost nobody does.** When someone asks what a
job costs in a named town, a page that answers with a real number from that town is often the only
candidate, and it gets cited. This is the same "informationally additive" test as everywhere else,
and it is far easier to pass locally than nationally.

## What is about to change, and what it means

Google has not meaningfully updated the local ranking algorithm in close to a decade, and
**proximity still carries the map pack**: which is why a mediocre shop four blocks away outranks a
strong one three miles out. The whole craft of local SEO is stretching that radius with geographic
and topical relevance.

Ask Maps put Gemini inside Google Maps in March 2026. It sits in a tab almost nobody uses, so it
changes nothing today. It reads as a test of whether Gemini can pick local businesses well, and if
that reaches the map pack, proximity stops deciding the order and starts deciding eligibility.
Whoever built a real presence then takes the radius instead of the neighbourhood.

That is the argument for doing this now, and the levers are the ones almost nobody pulls: review
language that names services and outcomes, a real page per category and per service rather than one
list, local press and local business-journal mentions, and video about the business on YouTube.

Multi-location chains sit at both ends of the barbell at once, 45 locations accumulate chamber
listings, business-journal coverage and hiring announcements as a side effect of size, and they are
assistants' default recommendation in every market they touch. A single-location business cannot
out-authority that and does not need to; it needs to be the specific answer for its own radius.
Cold brand recall is not a reasonable goal for one location, whatever a national agency's video says.
