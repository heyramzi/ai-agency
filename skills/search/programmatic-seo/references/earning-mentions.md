# Getting talked about somewhere other than your own site

The playbook for Phase 6. The measured reason this is the larger half, and the correlation numbers
behind it, are in [geo-signals.md](geo-signals.md); nothing here restates them.

The shape of the work: a model builds confidence in a brand from **consensus**, meaning the same
claim about you appearing in many places it trusts. One excellent page on your own domain is one
source. Ten third parties saying the same thing is a consensus, and consensus is what a probability
distribution rewards.

Three tiers, hardest and most valuable first.

## Tier 1. Third-party editorial

Industry publications, review sites, and the listicles and comparison posts on authoritative blogs.
The hardest mentions to earn and the exact page shape assistants cite.

**Find the pages that already decide your category.** Two reads, in order:

1. The cited-domains report for your topic in any AI visibility tool: the sites the assistants are
   already pulling from when they answer questions in your space. That is the target list.
2. The listicles that will be cited next. Search for `title:best` and `title:versus` in your niche
   with your brand name excluded, then filter for pages carrying real referring domains. A page
   with links and topical fit gets cited eventually whether or not it is cited today, so getting on
   it early is cheaper than getting on it late.

**Outreach that works is a swap or a story, never a request.** "Please add us to your list" reads
as rude and is refused; the same ask offered reciprocally, or attached to something the publisher
wants, lands. When somebody sends *you* that request, keep it: they have just told you which query
they think is worth ranking for, which is free keyword intelligence.

**The listicle arithmetic is worth doing before the outreach.** If every page in a set lists ten
brands and there are twenty pages, two hundred slots exist and the assistant returns five. Climbing
that list is a different job from entering it. You can also answer a listicle query without
building a listicle: publish ten specific claims about your own standing in the niche, each
provable, and let the model assemble the list.

## Tier 2. Communities

Reddit, Quora and niche forums. Reddit is among ChatGPT's most-cited domains and a foundational
training source, and decision-stage queries ("which should I buy", "is this worth it", "what is
best for me") are the fastest-growing category in AI search and the one Reddit answers.

Find the threads that already rank: pull Reddit's own organic keywords, filter to positions one to
five, and include your niche terms. Those threads are already being retrieved. Answer them
genuinely, where your product actually fits. A brand-name drop backfires, and the community detects
a corporate account immediately.

**Carry the dispute rather than resolving it.** The counter-argument comes from practitioners
watching the channel degrade: Reddit is being astroturfed, upvotes are bought, marketers arrived
the moment the trust did, and the platforms are already discounting it. Both readings are held by
credible people. The resolution both sides accept is that Reddit is one input and not the strategy,
and a brand that is only on Reddit has a single point of failure. Also note that a Reddit or Medium
page does not rank by existing: those pages need the same work as any other, and posting on a
high-authority domain is not a shortcut past it.

## Tier 3. Properties you own

YouTube, a podcast, LinkedIn, your directory listings. All crawled, all quotable, all fully under
your control, which makes this the tier to start on when the other two are slow.

**Directories and profiles are entity resolution, not link building.** Google Business Profile,
G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Product Hunt, the industry-specific directories. Consistent name, address
and description across all of them tells a model these signals point at one company; "XYZ Corp",
"XYZ Company" and "XYZ Inc." read as three. Wikipedia and Wikidata are the strongest version of
this where the notability bar is genuinely met, and a waste of months where it is not.

**LinkedIn is the B2B exception with a caveat.** It is heavily cited for professional queries, and
individual posts generate close to no search traffic and are cited far less than YouTube or Reddit.
Post there for the audience and the branded search it creates, not expecting the post itself to be
the cited source.

## YouTube deserves its own budget line

The strongest single correlation anyone has measured, and four independent sources reach the same
conclusion, including two platform vendors reading their own citation data. The mechanism is
ordinary: transcripts are text, they are long, they are conversational, and a large model was
trained on more than a million hours of them. A ten-minute video is a 2,000-word document already
phrased as natural-language answers.

Target **search hits, not viral hits.** A viral video spikes and dies once the algorithm exhausts
the interested audience. A search hit earns traffic from Google and YouTube search month after
month on an evergreen query, and those are the videos assistants pull from, because a video Google
already ranks for a query is a video its AI surfaces cite.

Find the topics by pulling the organic keywords that `youtube.com/watch` ranks for in Google,
filtering to the top three positions, and including your niche terms. That is a list of topics
where a video is the format Google has already decided the query wants.

Then the checklist, all five:

- **The keyword goes in the title**, plainly. Save the creativity for the thumbnail; the title
  carries the query and the thumbnail sells the click.
- **The description opens with a real summary** carrying the keyword in the first two lines.
- **Timestamps**, which become chapters and can be linked to directly from a search result.
- **Say the keyword out loud in the video.** Google parses audio, and its VP of Search has said so.
  A term that exists only in the title is not in the transcript, and the transcript is the document.
- **Match the format already ranking.** If tutorials own the SERP, make a tutorial.

Clean up the auto-generated transcript, at least on the brand names and the jargon. That transcript
is what gets read.

Where making the videos is not realistic, buy the mention: sponsorships and creator collaborations
put the brand into somebody else's transcript, which counts the same.

## Digital PR, the highest-leverage play on this list

Getting authoritative publications to talk about you. The version that works has one rule behind
it: **pitch the story the journalist wants to write, not the story you want written.** A new
product, a new colour, a funding round: not a story. There are a fraction as many journalists as
there were before 2020, they are overloaded, and a press release about your launch is deleted.

A worked campaign, for a luggage brand with no authority at all. Total cost about $100 and some
hours; result, roughly 60 links from national publications, and the brand started appearing in AI
answers and taking orders off the back of it. Five steps:

1. **Recon.** Read what the target journalists are actually writing about this month. Not your
   category, the adjacent one they cover. For a luggage brand that was air travel.
2. **Analyse.** Find or make the data. They booked trips on every major airline and tabulated every
   add-on charge into a spreadsheet. The story became "which airlines have the most hidden fees".
   Note that none of it was proprietary data and none of it was about suitcases.
3. **Infiltrate.** Build the list of journalists who have written on that subject before. Not a
   blast to every address you can find.
4. **Deploy.** A short email with the finding, the method, and the data attached.
5. **Score.** Count the placements, and reuse the ones that landed.

The published article says "new data from <brand> shows" and is otherwise not about you. **Do not
try to sell in it.** The campaign above sold two suitcases directly, which is not the point and
never was: it is buying the authority that makes you the named answer later. A piece that tries to
do both does neither.

Two variants of the same move. **Trendjacking**: attach a credible expert read to something already
in the news, which is how "that shredded Banksy is now worth double" became a national story with
an art appraiser's name on it. **The niche pivot**: an accountant who could never rank for
"accountant Melbourne" became the topical authority for accounting for OnlyFans creators, pitched
journalists a genuinely interesting tax angle, and built a multi-million dollar practice on a term
nobody was defending.

The free, low-effort version for a small operator: answer journalist queries on Source of Sources
or a similar service, fifteen minutes a day. It is a numbers game, perhaps one placement in a
hundred submissions, and it costs nothing.

## Case studies are the shape with no competition

Write ten to twenty of them, with the numbers in, and give away what happened rather than teasing
it. The reason is mechanical: a buyer describes their own situation to an assistant in a sentence
no keyword tool has ever recorded, "I run an agency doing $20M and I have this specific problem",
and the only document that matches is the case study of that exact situation. Nobody else in your
category has written it, because everybody withholds detail.

## Spam links that arrived on their own are not cleaned up

Building a system to clean them is work with no outcome. Since Penguin 4.0 every site accrues them,
and Google devalues them rather than counting them against the target; the disavow file is for
links *you* built or bought, and Google's own guidance pairs it with a manual action. Nothing else
reads the file, so a disavow does nothing for AI citation either.

Three checks replace the cleanup: a manual action in Search Console (the only alarm that matters,
and it is UI-only, no API), a sudden jump in referring domains, and the share of links the flagged
domains actually carry. Judge a domain on the traffic Google sends it, never on a spam score alone:
DataForSEO scores bing.com at 20 and every parked scraper at 50.
Measured on one consultancy domain, 6 Sep 2026: 28 of 50 referring domains flagged, carrying 32 of
440 links, against a domain spam score of 6.

## The scoreboard for this phase

Not links. Track whether new domains have started citing you that were not before, whether the
share of voice against the named competitors has moved, and whether what the assistants say about
you is accurate and positive. Being named badly is a loss, not a neutral result, and a single
negative theme can resurface dozens of times across branded answers until something specific
displaces it. Cadence and instrumentation: [measurement.md](measurement.md).
