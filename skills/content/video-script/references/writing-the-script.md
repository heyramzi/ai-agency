# Writing the long-form script

Bullets, the beat budget, the block structure, the why, the teach block, and the argument video. `SKILL.md` holds what the measurements decided.

## Every script ships as bullets


**A bullet is one sentence the way he'd say it aloud:** under 25 words, contracted, linked by "and", "so" and "because", talking to "you". It fails both ways. 21 Aug 2026: every bullet was 30 to 50 words and he couldn't record it ("I'm literally not understanding what I need to record"). 24 Sep 2026, the Claude Code course: clipped fragments ("Paste the line. Press return. Let it run.") and his verdict was it didn't sound natural.

| Too clipped | One spoken sentence | Too long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `- Settings, App Center, MCP Servers. The panel is new.` | `- Open Settings, then App Center, and you'll see a panel called MCP Servers that's new this month.` | `- This one does not get finished on camera, and the reason is the point rather than a skipped step, because...` |

- The save is the gate. `concepts script <id> --file` refuses a banned phrase or beats off the `spoken` register (16 to 34 words a sentence, contractions, chaining, "you") and names every hit. Rewrite what it names and save again.
- One bullet per beat, in spoken order; sub-bullets for parts, on-screen notes or an open question.
- No stage directions. "Land it:", "Say it plainly:", "The turn:" were rejected 19 Aug 2026 as a director talking to talent, and the talent is the author.
- 3 things are written in full, in quotes, past the word cap: the cold open, the closing ask, and any measured number, spoken exactly.
- Editorial notes stay prose outside the list.
- Spoken, not written. Out loud he runs 24.5 words a sentence against 7.5 written and chains at 2 to 3 times the written rate (`humanizer`, `references/voice-measured.md`). A run of short declaratives is the written cadence read out. Use the real noun and numbers with the artefact that proves them.
- Camera language outside, mechanics inside. On camera use the founder's words ("batches, sprints of one week", "billable time"); Points, the slipped tag and the space model are named only inside a teach block that's showing them. The why, failure, stakes and ask stay in camera language.

## The beat budget

**Budget in beats, one bullet each.** A word budget can only be spent by lengthening bullets, and that's how the 21 Aug 2026 script turned into prose. One beat is about 7 seconds on camera (179 bullets, 6.95s each, on a plan sized to 195 wpm); the how-to rewrite landed at 169. So 19 minutes is **158 to 185 beats**, the same as 3,600 to 4,200 words.

- Short of target means **missing beats**, never missing words: the exact click, the field name on screen, the number the viewer sees, the common mistake and its cost.
- Record the word count as a reading and never as a target. The teleprompter paces in beats per minute.

## Structure

| Block | Share | Arc | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold open | 0:00-0:30 | | `video-hooks.md` |
| The why | ~6% | Ordinary world, call | Why they need this and why now: a fear, named once and already true |
| The failure | ~6% | Refusal, ordeal | Your own, told in full, in the buyer's words |
| The stakes | ~5% | Cost of refusal | What the current way costs. One real number with its artefact |
| The mechanism | ~14% | The gift | The thing that makes it work, named. Lead with it before the promise |
| Build 1..n | ~50% | Road of trials | One idea per block, each ending in a state visible on screen |
| The proof | ~11% | Return changed | The artefact doing the thing. 2 configurations from one input beat one screenshot |
| The honest limit | ~4% | | Who this doesn't work for |
| The ask | last 20s | | One outbound ask: a named product, a price and one link |

The why is paid for out of the failure and the stakes. Before 19 Sep 2026 there was no why block and the other 2 did its job badly: a broken morning is evidence, and evidence means something only to someone who already agreed it matters. **Every build ends on a visible state change**; a block ending on a sentence is a paragraph, one ending on the board looking different is a beat.

## The why, and the viewer as hero

The author, 19 Sep 2026, on a Claude guide that opened straight into the broken morning: "We always should start with why when we record a video."

- The lever is fear, already true. Name what's broken today, load the consequence, stop and let them answer (lever table: `video-hooks.md`). An invented stake is the one thing that can cost the account.
- Spend emotion in 3 places only: the why, the stakes and the last beats of the proof. A build is teaching.
- One lever per block, named in the brief. The Claude guide: fear in the why, shame-release in the failure, loss aversion in the stakes, nothing through the 4 builds, identity split closing the proof.
- The viewer is the hero, you're the mentor: a scar and a gift, then out of the way ([storytelling](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/references/storytelling.md), "The 5 moves"). `reviewScript` counts pronouns per block. "Our proposal skill takes the CRM record we created" is the mentor doing the work; "your own record, the one you filed 2 minutes ago" is the same demo with the hero inside. On the 19 Sep guide, 2 of 10 blocks failed, both builds, 11 "our/my" against 4 "your".

| Arc position | Block | Owes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary world, call | The why | One object they own plus the clock on it |
| Refusal, ordeal | The failure | They already tried; your scar, 2 beats at most |
| Cost of refusal | The stakes | Cost per week, measured, with the artefact |
| The gift | The mechanism | The one portable idea, named once |
| Road of trials | Build 1..n | One failure per block, fixed on camera, and the idea restated |
| Return changed | The proof | The opening object replayed, changed; never a bare number |

**The why's beats** (11 at a 174-beat budget): the object they own now (the Monday morning, the chat window, a folder nobody else can open; pick one you can film twice, it returns at the end of the proof); what's already broken in their words from `conversion`; the consequence with a clock ("still true in 12 months", the one beat allowed to frighten, with a real number under it); the identity split, one sentence, letting them place themselves (telling them which side is patronising, `learned-patterns.md` 10 Sep 2026); the turn, one decision named and not yet explained. Then stop. A why that teaches has spent the mechanism's material.

## The teach block (53% of the script)

Read off Nick Saraev's `QoQBzR1NIqI` (4:10:42 long, 62,139 words in 35 chapters, 2,314,591 views on 502k subs and a 4.6x channel multiplier; read 23 Aug 2026). **Single source, no control test**; it's here because the alternative was nothing.

- One portable idea, restated at the close of every build. His 4 hours run on one loop, named at 00:39:35: give it the task, let it do it and make it verify. One idea restated 6 times holds longer than 6 ideas listed once. Name it in the mechanism block, point at it as each build lands.
- Open each block on its line, close on it. It carries a third to a half of the idea (a sentence holding the whole block is a slogan nobody says), returns in nearly the same words (a coached client closed on "we go where the money flows" 5 times), and returns on the beat where the build lands and not on the last beat, which is the seam. Source: Joseph Tsar on 31 Aug 2026, unmeasured.
- Draw the abstraction on the same screen (a ship with a stiff rudder for why the instruction file steers everything; a staircase from 80% to 100% for why speed beats one-shot accuracy). The `whiteboard` skill builds them. Drawing on the shared screen isn't b-roll, so it survives.
- Define each term the moment it appears, in objects they own: "An IDE is a file explorer plus a notepad plus ChatGPT, in one." 4 seconds each.
- Show it failing once per build, uncut, and fix it on camera (an error read out, 987 of 989 records processed). A block that ends working after showing it not working is a state change twice over.
- When the block is a verdict on a tool, make the tool produce the number. Theo's memory video asked Claude Code what it stored: 3 writes per read, 26 of 45 files never opened. Ask before you've decided, and only what the tool can answer from its own data; a demo confirming an announced verdict reads as re-enactment.
- The build is real work out of the business, never a toy: his agency site, a PandaDoc replacement and a classifier over 989 real emails at 0.36s each. It's the only version that can carry.
- Cost in money and minutes, never a percentage: $17 a month; 35 minutes without a plan against 10 with. Never compute on screen.
- Answer the dating problem in one sentence in the mechanism block: the screens will look different by the time you watch, what matters is where to find the current version (00:04:36).
- Dictate the prompts and say why once: about 60 words a minute typed against about 200 spoken.
- A guide is navigated, not watched: one chapter per block, named for the state reached ("the Project that stops the re-explaining"), never the feature.

## The body as an argument

A thesis video has no screen or click; its failure mode is a chronology. Source: theyo, `cZtvi1YzNII` (22:38 long, 4,660 words at 206 wpm, read 27 Aug 2026). **No performance evidence** (8,023 views a day after publish, no median), so these are candidates; it also publishes no chapters, which is a data point and gives no licence.

- Each link in the chain ends on what was still missing, and that lack is why the next link exists. Clippy 1997 knew nothing about intent; Salesforce left the user carrying intent between tools; ChatGPT understood but couldn't act; plugins could act on weak models; MCP connected everything and settled nothing about permission. Name the lack first, the date second. It's the open loop run 5 times.
- Open on a dated object that returns as the answer. The paperclip comes back twice, changed. In an argument video the motif is the answer, so an object that can't come back as one is the wrong object. Open on what the room already dismissed and the shock is low and the proof fast.
- Find the scene someone staged: Salesforce's "End of Software" launch party, then paid actors picketing a competitor's conference, carries the claim with the irony attached.
- Caveat the number in place, once, then keep using it: Airtable $11bn in 2021 against a $1.3bn acquisition in Aug 2026 with $480m ARR; AI doesn't explain it alone.
- Ask the title question twice and refuse the cheap answer both times (midpoint: more complicated than a big yes; close: no, an evolution of the format).
- Fence the prediction out loud, once ("now I'm going to play fortune teller"); the register switch is the permission.
- Close by giving permission, then ask. Release the viewer, then the one outbound ask in the last 15 seconds. Ending on the bad news converts worse, and the ask doesn't move to fix it.

Done when the shape matches the video (build or argument), the beat count is inside 158 to 185, and the save accepted the script.
