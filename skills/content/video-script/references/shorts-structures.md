# Short structures, note shape and platform facts

How a whole Short is built between the hook and the CTA, how its note is shaped, and what YouTube Shorts does with it. Hook rules are in `video-hooks.md`.

## The skeleton's source

@kallawaymarketing (Kane Kallaway), Instagram, scraped 6 Aug 2026: 30 posts with captions plus whisper transcripts of his 4 most-liked reels. Not control-tested (one account, 24.5k likes on the top post, about 300 median, and likes accumulate with age). Import structures, never register: "scientifically impossible to skip" is a claim the truth rule bans and `humanizer` rejects. The skeleton survives translation into calm, evidenced speech.

## The skeleton, in all 30 posts

1. Outcome hook, one line. "This is how you [outcome]" or "Here's how to [outcome] in [true compressed time]". No setup before it.
2. Name the method, only if you'd say the name anyway. "This is called the 532 Method." A name gives the recap and the CTA something to point at. "I call my fix the one library rule" is a label made up for a checker; leave it out.
2b. The objection sentence, optional. Say the doubt the viewer already holds, quoted from `conversion`: "This is the part that sounds like it needs a bigger team than you have." Never the version that promises the video's own value.
3. 3 or 4 numbered steps, each one concept plus a carrier: a worked example, an analogy or a named person. Never a bare definition. Count them out loud, "step 1, step 2, step 3", the way the top band does.
4. A flip near the middle (below).
5. Recap in one line, naming the parts.
6. Reply-trigger CTA with a named artefact that exists before the post goes out. "Comment 'formats' and I'll send you the database of all 22 formats."

Kallaway runs 240 to 435 words, 90 to 150 seconds. Under 60 seconds, keep the skeleton and cut steps and keep the carriers: 2 steps with examples beat 4 of definitions.

```
HOOK      This is how [outcome the viewer wants], in [true compressed time].
NAME      It's called the [Named Method]. [count] steps.
STEP 1    [Concept]. [Worked example in the viewer's world.]
STEP 2    [Concept]. [Example or analogy.]
~25s FLIP [What part 1 fixes, as a fact.] [What breaks in part 2.]
STEP 3    [Concept]. [Your own measured number, shown on screen.]
RECAP     [Part], [part] and [part]. That's the whole method.
CTA       Comment "[trigger]" and I'll send you [artefact that exists].
```

The flip carries a fact across the boundary, never an announcement. "But knowing part one is not the point, here's how you actually do it" is a negation pivot on a throat-clearing opener: delete it and nothing stands. On a list-shaped Short the flip is the demotion: "[Step 1] and [step 2] do nothing on their own. The one that moves [the number] is the last." Rank items by what carries the result and put that one last; where items are equal, drop the move.

3 hook surfaces, and the spoken one ranks last (text, then visual, then spoken; "99/100 read the text hook first"). Kallaway's "6 Hook Power Words" map to ours: subject is always "you", then action, objective, and contrast (his most important word, our shock and curiosity-gap rows). His "Triple Hook" (context, lean, contrarian snapback) is a negation pivot by another name, so the one-per-script cap holds.

## Retention structures for the body

All one idea: open a loop, pay it off and open the next before attention runs out.

- Modern story arc: drop in at 70/100, spike to 90 in the first seconds, then peak-release-peak every 10 to 15 seconds.
- Mid flip: attention span is about 25 seconds. Part 1 covers what it is and part 2 covers how to do it, with the flip on a contrast word once.
- Dopamine loop: stakes (a character, something at risk, urgency), big question, head fake, re-hook. Tactics per beat: [storytelling](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/references/storytelling.md), [addiction-loop](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/references/storytelling/addiction-loop.md). Read it before a Short whose body is a story.
- Delayed reward: the mid flip in list shape. A counted list pays a little at each item, so demote what's delivered just before the last. Source: TikTok @lacedmedia, 12 Sep 2026, 10.1k views, unmeasured.
- Anticipation loading: deliver a useful answer, then open the next specific question. Use the promise-and-payoff variant below when the beats depend on each other.
- Small effort, big reward: frame the solution as tiny and the reward as large, by choosing small solutions.

## Promise and payoff, an optional variant

Source: [Loïc Bourget's reel](https://www.instagram.com/reels/DdmLvdTIdyR/), inspected 5 Oct 2026. It diagrams a hook, promise, 3 content parts separated by 2 open loops, then a CTA. One creator's explanation, no controlled retention or conversion evidence; test the structure without importing its conversion claim or generic suspense lines.

Use it when answering one question naturally exposes the next. The anchor still decides the shape; a direct tip needs no extra promise sentence, fixed count of parts or manufactured cliffhanger.

- Hook and promise: name the problem and the concrete result the viewer will learn or see. One line can do both; "I'll explain in a few seconds" adds no result.
- Content beats: each delivers a useful fact, example or action. Its transition names a remaining question, constraint or consequence that the following beat answers. "The next part is even crazier" supplies none.
- Payoff and ask: deliver the opening's result in the video, then tie one action to using it. A promised answer stays in the video; an existing CTA artefact can extend it. Use the platform's ask placement, without a padded sign-off.

Keep a promise-to-payoff map in the brief: opening promise or transition question → the line or visible proof that answers it. These notes stay outside CutKit's spoken body.

Review the finished draft with these pass/fail checks:

1. The opening names a concrete result and its payoff appears before the video ends.
2. Each open loop names a specific unknown, and a later beat resolves it; no generic teaser substitutes for substance.
3. Each content beat delivers something useful before creating another gap. Removing suspense language still leaves a coherent explanation.
4. The single CTA follows from the result, and any offered artefact exists. Nothing promised in the video requires a comment or DM to receive it.

Fix failures in at most 2 passes; remove or narrow a promise that would require invented evidence. Report any remaining gap separately from CutKit's grade. When testing, hold the other bricks fixed and compare retention near transitions with qualified actions (such as resource requests or sign-ups) separately; views alone establish neither retention nor conversion.

## Brick decomposition

A short is 11 bricks in 5 categories: Format; Idea (topic, seed, substance); Hook (text, visual, spoken); Script (story structure, CTA); Edit (layout, elements). Hold 6, change 1: borrow the bricks proven in the niche and invent on exactly one, or a beginner's first 50 pieces teach nothing. Name the failing brick before proposing a fix ("the hook is fine, the substance is thin"). A video tests one named change, stated before publishing. His volume advice (3 to 7 a week) is a coaching heuristic; our control series found cadence is a floor and no strategy (`competitor-evidence.md`, Ross Harkness published every Sunday while his median fell 3x).

## The note shape

Since 1 Oct 2026 only the clean read goes to CutKit; the rest rides on the Socials task.

- `CLEAN READ` sits directly under the title. He shoots off the note, so the teleprompter version must be on screen when it opens. A note without one gets one written from its cued lines. Everything else (metadata row, hook, re-check block, TikTok cut, cued version) sits below a `---`.
- `INTRO`, `MEAT` and `OUTRO` with timings; the MEAT is bullets. The speaker reads the first and last lines as written and says the middle his own way, so a scripted middle flattens the register. The closing line is shared word for word with the pinned post and first DM, so rewording it on camera breaks surfaces that deliberately say one thing.
- A conditional qualifies the audience and opens no gap. Batch 2, 9 Sep 2026: 7 notes written to that sentence alone gave 7 flat openings, no named lever, no variants, 3 over the 8-to-15-word budget, 2 text hooks copied from the title. Before filing, the opening has a named lever, 3 rated variants and 3 agreeing surfaces.
- Under the timings: the metadata row, `Lives at:`, word count against target pace, tags, and a `RE-CHECK BEFORE SHOOTING` block holding what goes wrong if it's said differently. Read the newest note in the live batch first, because corrections accumulate there.

## YouTube Shorts facts

- Algorithm: a seed cohort tests it; strong early engagement (past 5 seconds) triggers exploit and it scales. A Short can take off as late as day 4. CTR plays almost no role. It loses its push after about 28 to 30 days (September 2025).
- Signals in order: completion (70%+ means aggressive promotion), loop rate (average viewed over 100% means loops), comments and shares over likes, viewed vs swiped away (75%+ good, under 60% test a new opening now, under 50% broken hook), satisfaction.
- Specs: max 3 minutes (Oct 2024); sweet spot 15 to 60 seconds; bimodal peaks at 13s and 60s; 9:16 at 1080x1920; captions essential and a visual change every 3 seconds. Quick tips 15 to 20s, tutorials 25 to 40s. Content ID music blocks 1 to 3 minute Shorts worldwide; TikTok watermarks are suppressed.
- Discovery: about 40 title characters show, so 4 to 6 words and front-load keywords; 3 to 5 hashtags; the first 125 characters of the description preview. Shorts appear in YouTube Search and Google mobile. Thumbnails don't matter in the feed.
- Views: any playback is a view since March 2025 (totals up about 30%, loops count again), so read Engaged Views.
- Money: revenue is pooled, the creator keeps 45%, and each music track cuts the share (1 track 50%, 2 tracks 33%). 1M views is about $10 to $70 (finance up to $300). Shorts are 22% of YouTube ad revenue (2025).
- Long-form: audience overlap is about 10% and related-video conversion under 1%, but channels using both grow 40 to 60% faster (MacDannyGun: 670K subs, 10x long-form views); no cannibalization in the US (Tubefilter, Feb 2025). Repurpose self-contained "aha" moments; hook-value-CTA clips beat unbroken ones by 74%; one 20 to 30 minute video yields 10 to 20 Shorts. Test hooks as Shorts, then make the winners long-form.
