# Planning a video's b-roll

The first artefact of any run, before a single clip exists. SKILL.md holds the three verdicts -
b-roll, the speaker's face, the speaker's screen - and this holds how much, where, and the arithmetic that checks
your own judgment after the fact.

## How much, and where

**Coverage is an outcome, never a target.** Every beat is decided on its own by the value test
above, one at a time, and the percentage is what you get when that is done honestly. A number
decided first turns the plan into a quota, and a quota is filled with exactly the clips this skill
exists to prevent: on-palette, on-clock, and saying what the sentence already said.

So the order is fixed. Judge each sentence. Then compute total clip seconds over composition
seconds, put the number at the top of the plan, and use it only as a **check on your own judgment**:

- Under about 40 percent on a talking-head explainer, go back and look for beats you were lazy
  about. Do not add clips to reach a number. If the second pass finds nothing, the number stands and
  you say why in the plan.
- Over about 60 percent, the person selling it has disappeared behind the speaker's own graphics. Cut the
  weakest ones, starting with any clip whose value-test answer needed more than one sentence.

**The band does not apply to every video, and pretending it does is the failure.** A product tour
where the speaker is demonstrating on screen is mostly screen by construction: a graphic over real evidence
argues with the evidence behind it, so the honest coverage there is low and forcing it to 40 would
damage the video. State the band you are actually working to, against the runtime it applies to,
and say what the rest of the video is. A per-video `plan.md` is where it goes:
thirteen of its twenty-two minutes are screen, so the target is stated against the other nine.

Balance is a separate constraint from coverage and it is the one that gets missed:

- **No stretch over about 45 seconds with no b-roll.** That is where a viewer leaves.
- **No stretch over about 25 seconds with no face.** That is where they stop trusting it.
- **Never two clips back to back with no face between them** unless the second one is the payoff of
  the first. Two graphics in a row read as a montage and the read underneath goes unheard.
- **Front-load nothing.** A lesson with six clips in the first two minutes and none in the last two
  has not been planned, it has been worked through in order until attention ran out.

Build in plan order, not in the order the lines were handed over. If only some beats are being built
this session, say which ones in the plan and leave the rest with their verdict written.


**The line the speaker pastes is not the line that is spoken.** The speaker copies a scene out of the editor and the
copy brings the ignored words with it, struck through in grey and absent from the export. On M0 L1
that cost one beat a sentence and another every word of it, its surviving statement being the next
paragraph, which named a fourth item. The paste points at a place in the cut; it is not the script.
Check `getComputedStyle(...).textDecorationLine` per text node before building anything.
