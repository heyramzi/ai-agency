# Story locks

Six sentence-level moves that hold attention. They are word swaps rather than structures, which is
what makes them usable late: a finished script can be walked once per lock and come out tighter
without a beat moving. The structure they sit inside belongs to `storytelling`, and the four-beat
cycle they pace is its
[`references/addiction-loop.md`](../../../../content/writing/storytelling/references/addiction-loop.md).

They apply to a Short, to a long-form body and to a post equally, so `video-script` reads this file
rather than restating it.

**Contrast is the engine under the other five**, and it is number 6. A name contrasts with the
explanation the viewer expected. A certain sentence contrasts with a hedged one. Hearing your own
thought out loud contrasts with hearing a stranger's. A warning contrasts with advice. If only one
of the six is going to be used on a draft, use that one.

**Two of them collide with rules this registry already enforces, and the registry wins.** The
collisions are named in place rather than smoothed over: the hedge rule under lock 2, and the
banned phrase bank under lock 5.

## 1. Term branding: name the thing, then it cannot be left

Naming a concept makes it feel like a thing rather than a description, and a name the viewer does
not recognise opens a small question they have to stay for. The mechanism is the labeling effect.
Hormozi's value equation and Kevin Kelly's thousand true fans are both ideas that would have been
forgotten as explanations.

**This registry already does it and calls it something else.** `shorts-structures.md` step 2 is
"name the method", and `video-script`'s ship list requires "one portable idea, named in the
mechanism". What this adds is the reason it holds retention rather than just aiding recall, and one
caution.

- A name is not a factual claim, so the truth rule leaves it free. What is not free is naming a
  method that does not exist, or that nobody has run.
- **Do not name everything.** Contrarian ideas and real frameworks earn a name; a step in a list
  does not. Naming three things per Short turns a script into a glossary and each name is worth
  less than the last.
- The name has to be countable or concrete enough to point at in the recap and to carry the
  artefact CTA. `storytelling`'s motif rule is the long-form version: one object, returning
  changed.

## 2. Embedded truths: say it as true or do not say it

A hedge is a fork in the road. "If you try this" hands the viewer a decision about whether the
sentence applies to them, and the decision costs more attention than the sentence returns. "When
you try this" removes the fork. Every `maybe`, `might`, `could`, `probably`, `some people` is a
small exit door.

| Hedged | Embedded |
| --- | --- |
| This might work because… | The reason this works is… |
| You might notice… | Once you see it, you cannot unsee it |
| Some agencies could be making this mistake | The mistake most agencies make is this |
| It could be worth trying | Do this first |

`humanizer` pattern 24 already bans over-qualifying and Ogilvy rule 9 already bans softening a
known answer, so this lock adds the mechanism rather than a new rule: the hedge does not merely
read weakly, it is where the viewer leaves.

**The collision, and how it resolves.** Two house rules require a hedge, and both survive intact:
Ogilvy rule 9 permits honest uncertainty, and `storytelling`'s caveat rule requires the objection
to the strongest number to be said out loud. Neither is hedging in this sense.

> **Hedge the provenance of a claim. Never hedge the instruction.**

"This is one channel with no control band, so treat it as a hypothesis" is provenance, and it is
required. "This might help you a bit" is an instruction with the confidence removed, and it is the
exit door. The caveat rule is provenance said in one sentence at the point the number lands, after
which the number keeps being used at full strength.

## 3. Thought narration: say the thought they are having

Say out loud what the viewer is thinking at that moment, in their words, before they act on it. It
buys two things at once: they conclude you know the subject, because you knew what they were
thinking, and they now need your answer to their own question rather than to yours.

- "You are probably thinking this only works if you already have a team."
- "The question you are asking here is what happens when the client says no."
- "If you run a 4-person agency, the objection you have right now is the price."

**This is the strongest form of the house's oldest rule.** `humanizer`, "On camera, every I is a
you", asks the script to point at the viewer; thought narration points at the inside of their head.
It is also the general case of the objection sentence in
[`shorts-structures.md`](shorts-structures.md) step 2b, which is thought narration used once, on a
doubt, right after the method is named.

**The thought is harvested, not invented.** `customer-voice` holds it in verbatim form from 367
recorded calls, which is the difference between naming the doubt an agency founder actually has and
naming the average doubt. A guessed thought that misses reads worse than no thought narration at
all, because the viewer now knows the video is not for them.

Cadence: after a major point, at a transition, a few times per video. Every block turns it into a
tic.

## 4. Negative frames: a warning outranks an offer

Loss aversion. People are about twice as motivated to avoid a loss as to seek an equivalent gain
(Kahneman and Tversky, 1979), so a viewer who thinks they may already be making the mistake stays
in a way a viewer offered an improvement does not.

**The negative flip** is the mechanical version: take the point, invert it.

| Positive | Flipped |
| --- | --- |
| Here is how to grow on YouTube | This is what is killing your YouTube growth |
| Post more consistently | This posting habit is costing you subscribers |
| Use this hook format | Stop writing your hooks like this |

Softer frames work on the same instinct: "you are making this harder than it has to be" says
something is wrong without naming a fault.

**Three constraints, and the first is a hard one.**

- **A negative frame is not a negation pivot.** The pivot is "it is not a project, it is a wish",
  defining a thing by denying another, and it stays capped at one per script by `SKILL.md` and by
  `humanizer` pattern 9. A negative frame is a warning about a real cost and does not use the
  see-saw construction at all. Flipping a point into the negative is not permission to pivot.
- **The mistake has to cost something real.** Warning about a harmless habit is manufactured
  urgency, which the doctrine bans and which the truth rule already covers. If the honest cost
  is small, say the small number.
- **Do not flip everything.** A script of nothing but warnings is a fear register, and `voice-dna`
  rejects it. The frame is a lever to reach for when a good point is not landing.

## 5. Loop openers: the transition is where they leave

A viewer's attention runs on a timer. The hook flips it once; every section boundary is where it
runs out. A loop opener is the sentence that closes the block and opens the next question in the
same breath, which is the re-hook beat in `storytelling`,
[`references/addiction-loop.md`](../../../../content/writing/storytelling/references/addiction-loop.md),
said at sentence scale.

**Cadence, and the source's number did not survive contact with the corpus.** He teaches one loop
opener every 20 to 30 seconds on a Short and every 60 to 90 seconds on long-form. Measured 29 Aug
2026 across 339 punctuated long-form transcripts, the niche's median gap between loop openers is
**230 seconds**, its 10th percentile is 85 and its 75th is 377. His number is about 3x tighter than
anything that ships in this niche.

So the house target is **no stretch past 300 seconds**, which is still tighter than 90% of the
corpus, and `video-script`, `scripts/story_metrics.py` fails a script only past 831 seconds, the
niche's own 75th percentile. On a Short the ~25-second flip in `shorts-structures.md` stands
unchanged: it was never measured either, but a 60-second piece with one turn in it is a different
claim from a 19-minute one with twelve.

**The collision, and it is the important part of this file.** The source's own phrase bank is
mostly banned here. `here's the thing`, `it turns out`, `here's the problem` and `the truth is` are
in `vibe-kit/packages/lint/data/slop-words.js` as throat-clearing openers, and "here is what nobody
talks about" is the `the-part-nobody` rule, the unclaimed-insight preamble. The ban is right and it
does not weaken the re-hook, because it catches exactly the version of the move that carries no
information.

> **The test: delete the transition. If a question or a fact is still standing, it was a re-hook.
> If the sentence disappears, it was throat-clearing.**

"And this is where it gets crazy" fails: nothing is left. "That fixes the reporting, and it is also
what broke the invoicing two weeks later" passes: the new question is in the clause.

House-legal openers, all of which name the next question rather than advertising it:

- "That fixes X. It also breaks Y, which is the next thing."
- "Which would have worked, except the client had already signed."
- "And that is where the second number comes in: [number]."
- "So the reporting is solved. The invoicing is not, and this is why."
- "Most people stop at that step. The step after it is where the [n] hours actually go."

Each one closes and opens on one clause, and each one carries a fact across the boundary. That is
also what stops a loop opener becoming bait: the question opened here gets answered in the block
that follows, not in the next video.

## 6. Contrast words: split the haymaker

Curiosity is a gap between what somebody expects and what is true, and a contrast word is what
builds the gap inside a single sentence. `but` is the strongest one. `actually` says the first
belief was wrong. `instead` redirects. `except` carves out the case that breaks the rule. `yet`
holds two things that should not both be true.

The tactic runs on the finished draft. **Go to the sentences that make the piece's main points, and
try to split each one in half: lean the viewer one way, then turn them.** Not "the tool saves four
hours a week", but "the tool saves four hours a week, and the agency that installed it lost six to
the training".

Three limits:

- **Not every sentence.** Overused, `but` reads as friction and the piece feels like it is arguing
  with itself. The main points only.
- **`it turns out` is banned here** by the slop list, along with the rest of lock 5's collision. The
  other five contrast words are ordinary English and are not on it.
- **A contrast word is not a licence to pivot.** "Not X, but Y" is still the negation pivot, still
  capped at one, and this lock is the most common way a draft grows a second one.

## What happened when the six were measured

One script put all six over 627 videos
on 13 channels, winner band against control band inside each channel, on 29 Aug 2026. Ten of 13 is
the threshold a sign test clears at that n.

| Lock | Axis | Held in | The niche's own p25 / p50 / p75 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term branding | term brands per 10 min | 7 of 13 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| Embedded truths | hedged instructions per 1k | 9 of 13 | 0 / 0.66 / 3.23 |
| Thought narration | per 10 min | 6 of 13 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| Negative frames | per 10 min | 5 of 12 | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| Loop openers | per 10 min | 7 of 13 | 0.34 / 0.84 / 1.91 |
| Contrast words | contrast sentences | 6 of 10 | 10.8% / 13.9% / 17.1% |

**Not one of them is a lever on reach**, which is the fifth replication of the finding `SKILL.md`
opens on and the first one that covers craft rather than construction. Embedded truths comes
closest, and it points at *less* hedging in winners rather than at more technique.

Two things the table is worth anyway. **Four of the six are unused in this niche**: the median
video contains zero term brands, zero thought narration and zero negative frames, so using them is
doing something three quarters of the field does not, on a channel whose thesis is that craft
decides who stays. And **it puts numbers under two house rules that were taste**: the
one-negation-pivot cap lands on the niche's p75 to p90 (0.18 per 1k words, about 0.7 pivots in a
4,000-word script), and 75% of the niche never uses a banned throat-clearing transition at all.

**The instrument has a bounded recall and says so.** It is a fixed phrase list, so it sees a loop
opener written the way this niche writes them and misses a fresh one, and it cannot tell a hedge
from a hedge being quoted in order to be criticised. Every count is a floor and every cadence
number an upper bound on the true gap.
