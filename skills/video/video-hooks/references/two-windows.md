# Two windows, two jobs

The 1-to-3-second Shorts window and the 30-second long-form open are different problems with different evidence behind them. Both are written out here.

## Two windows, two jobs

| Format | Window | Budget | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shorts, Reels, TikTok** | first **1 to 3 seconds** | 8 to 15 words | Stop the swipe. One claim, no setup. |
| **Long-form** | first **30 seconds** | 65 to 85 words | Earn the next minute. Claim, then credibility, then promise. |

**The word budgets above are a slower register than the category runs, and that is deliberate.**
Measured 11 Aug 2026 across 20 long-form videos from three AI-agency channels, 13.5 hours:
**204 to 262 words per minute, mean 232.** At that pace a 30-second open is about 115 words, not
65 to 85. Holding the tighter budget means speaking slower and saying fewer things, which is the
register; it is not an accident to be corrected upward. Method and per-video numbers belong in
your own transcript corpus.

Three more things that pass replicated across those 20 and are worth knowing before writing:

- **18 of 20 open on a claim and none of those says hello first.** The two that open chatty are
  the same creator's, and both sit at the bottom of his view counts in the set. Independent
  support for the banned hook shapes above.
- **The close is one formula in 19 of 20**: recap, free resource, paid step, next-video handoff,
  signoff, which stacks two or three asks. One creator runs the same closing sentence in six of
  seven videos. The single-ask close this skill already requires is the differentiator, not a
  constraint to apologise for.
- **The first call to action does not wait for the end.** One channel's median lands at 12% of
  runtime and its three-hour flagship plugs at 1%. Holding every ask to the last 30 seconds is a
  choice, not the norm.

**A counted promise is a contract.** An open that says "three things everyone has to be doing" owes
the viewer three beats they can count, each one named on screen. Breaking it is the most common
failure in the borrowed-clip format: the number buys the click and the body then wanders through
commentary, so the viewer leaves holding one idea and a sense of having been managed. Either
enumerate on delivery, or make the promise without the number.

**Refusing the category's own promise is the cheapest credibility available**, and it is missing
from the lever table because it is not an emotional lever, it is a trust move. In the strongest
opening measured, the creator states inside 30 seconds that he will not promise a revenue figure
in a week, and that the people who do are setting you up to fail. It costs one sentence, it needs
no number, and it only works in a niche where everyone else is making that promise. Check that
they are before spending the sentence.

For the short window: `references/shorts-playbook.md` for the algorithm and specs. The metric that matters is viewed versus swiped away. **Above 75% is good, below 50% means the hook is broken**, not the video.

For the long window: the four jobs below, then `SKILL.md`'s execution steps. The vendored
long-form hook writer and its retention guide were removed on 29 Aug 2026 with the rest of the
vendor files; `mechanisms.md` and `gap-shapes.md` carry what this skill actually uses.

### The long open has a fifth job the Short does not: make the viewer feel safe

**BRENS: big, relatable, easy, new, safe.** Score a long-form open against the five and take the
most you can get honestly. Four of them restate levers already in the table above (big is scale,
relatable is the tribal callout, new is FOMO, easy is the named method). **Safe is the one this
skill was missing**, and it only applies past about thirty minutes: the viewer is deciding whether
to spend an hour, and the open has to tell them the hour will not be wasted. It is answered by
naming who is talking and what they will walk away with, in one sentence, and it costs nothing in
honesty.

Safe is also why the promise has to be about the **topic rather than the tool**. Say inside the
open that the method holds if they use the other tool, and the viewer stops calculating whether
their stack disqualifies them. This is the same trust move as refusing the category's promise
above: one sentence, no number, and it widens the audience the piece can pay off for.

Single source, no control test: one channel's transcript, read once. It sits below anything
measured on your own channel.

### The hook outline: borrow the shape, not the subject

When an opening on another channel clearly worked, condense it to a **hook outline**, meaning the
intro written out beat by beat plus a one-line-per-section outline of the rest. Then pour your own
substance into that skeleton. This is `idea-mining`'s remix rule applied at the level of the open:
the structure is one brick, and holding it constant while changing the topic is the highest-hit-rate
remix available. Two constraints carry over unchanged, and they are the ones that get dropped: every
fact underneath the borrowed shape has to be yours and measured, and the winner still goes through
`humanizer` against a real speech sample. A borrowed structure in a borrowed voice is the failure
this produces.
