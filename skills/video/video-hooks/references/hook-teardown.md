# One creator's hooks, taken apart

Not a library. Nothing here is a bank of hooks to draw from, and reading it that way is the one
mistake it can cause. It is a teardown of one creator: his complete hook doctrine, his own hook
document verbatim, and 475 of his openings measured against his own control band. Read the
measurement first, because it changes what the rest of the file is for.

## Provenance, and how much to trust it

Source: **Mino (@mino.mp4)**, TikTok-first personal-brand teaching, 531 posts between
25 Sep 2022 and 27 Aug 2026, 41.5M lifetime views, median 13,100. He coaches creators on hooks
and sells a content programme, so the hook advice is his product rather than an aside.

Pulled 28 Aug 2026 off the channel's own catalogue:

| What | How | Where it landed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 531 posts with views, likes, comments, duration | `tiktok.ts mino` | `data/mino/tiktok.json` |
| 504 transcripts, word-level timings | `tiktok-transcripts.ts mino --all` | `data/mino/tiktok-transcripts/` |
| Text hooks off the first frames, top 60 | `tiktok-text-hooks.ts mino --top 60` | the same files, `textHook` |

**TikTok publishes its own caption track with word-level timings**, which no other surface in the
directory does. That is why the first 1.5 seconds is a quoted object here rather than a paraphrase,
and it is the reason this creator was worth pulling at all.

Two honesty notes, the same two that govern the Kallaway corpus in
[`shorts-structures.md`](shorts-structures.md):

1. **He is off-ICP.** He teaches creators; this channel sells to agency founders. Nothing here is an
   idea pipe, and `idea-mining` should not read this file. What transfers is construction.
2. **Import the structures, never the register.** His lines run on "fucking crazy", "dogshit",
   "literally life-changing". `voice-dna` rejects all of it and the truth rule in `SKILL.md` bans
   the unfalsifiable half. Every shape below survives translation into calm evidenced speech; none
   of the intensifiers needs to come with it.

Unlike that corpus, this one **is banded**, which is what the next section is.

## The finding: none of it separates his own bands

475 openings carry a caption inside the first 1.5 seconds. Split on **era-adjusted breakout**,
views over the median of the 40 videos nearest in time, so four years of follower growth cannot
pose as technique:

- **Winners**: 134 videos at 2.0x their era or better. His own threshold, incidentally: he tells
  viewers to treat any video at 2x their average as the signal worth pattern-matching.
- **Controls**: 221 videos between 0.5x and 1.5x.

Sixteen construction features were counted on the first sentence of each. **Not one separates the
bands at p<0.05**, on a test where sixteen comparisons would be expected to throw up roughly one
significant result by chance alone.

| Feature | Winners | Controls | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opens "here's" | 15% | 10% | 0.16 |
| Opens with I / my | 19% | 13% | 0.11 |
| Opens with you / your / if you | 10% | 8% | 0.37 |
| Opens on a question | 3% | 7% | 0.12 |
| Second person anywhere | 52% | 46% | 0.23 |
| First person anywhere | 49% | 54% | 0.40 |
| A digit | 43% | 38% | 0.35 |
| Negation or prohibition | 25% | 25% | 0.96 |
| An extremity word ("exactly", "only", "literally") | 34% | 28% | 0.27 |
| Names a problem or a pain | 7% | 10% | 0.30 |
| Promises a method ("how to", "how I") | 17% | 10% | 0.07 |
| Time or duration bound | 20% | 21% | 0.88 |
| A niche keyword | 48% | 46% | 0.77 |

Length does not separate them either: 9.9 words against 10.2 inside the first 1.5 seconds, 21.6
against 24.0 in the first sentence. Nor does runtime, at 83 seconds against 82.

**Read that the way this skill reads every band comparison: a trait present in both bands is house
style, and copying it buys nothing.** Every shape in the rest of this file is house style. They
are how he writes, they are not why any particular video worked.

This is the third independent replication of the finding `SKILL.md` opens on. Matt Gray's hits and
flops open identically across 27 transcripts; 627 transcripts across 13 channels put the winner
band at 107 words against the control's 106. Now one creator's 475 short-form openings, on the
platform where the hook is supposed to be everything, and where he is the one selling the hook
course. **The hook decides whether people stay. It does not decide how many arrive.**

## The four matched pairs, which are the cheapest way to see it

The corpus contains near-duplicate openings that landed in different bands. These are as close to
a natural control as an observational corpus gets, because construction is held still by
similarity and everything else is free to vary.

| | Winner | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The same sentence, twice** | "here's how to edit your TikTok video so you go viral" — **3.3x**, 33,700, Nov 2022 | "Here's how to Edit Your TikTok videos so you go viral." — **0.8x**, 7,556, Mar 2023 |
| **Same month, same subject** | "Can't afford to pay rent for me and my girlfriend." — **246x**, 3.1M, Jun 2026 | "I can't afford to pay rent for me and my girlfriend but I don't have the balls to ask her to split the rent with me" — **1.0x**, 12,000, Jun 2026 |
| **His own signature construction** | "here's how I add captions to my TikTok so they go viral every single time" — **106x**, 1.8M, Jan 2023 | "…is exactly how I word my TikTok hook so I go viral every single time" — **1.1x**, 19,500, Feb 2023 |
| **The framework, textbook-executed** | "Stop deleting your videos." — **28.5x**, 311,100, Oct 2022 | "In the next 60 seconds I'm going to guarantee that you never struggle to come up with viral content ideas ever again." — **1.0x**, 13,600, May 2024 |

The last row is the one to sit with. The control is his own framework executed perfectly: a
duration bound, a guarantee, an extreme result, the word "you", an objection-proof promise. It did
its channel's median. The winner is four words with no mechanism in it at all.

Thirteen such pairs exist above 0.62 similarity, and the pattern holds across all of them.

## What he says the hook is made of

Kept because it is the best-articulated statement of short-form hook craft in the corpus, and
because a shape you can name is a shape you can audit. Not because any of it is a lever.

**The three-part hook** (Jun 2023, 631k views). Target viewer, stated so specifically the right
person feels addressed; the dream result for that viewer; the specific solution that gets them
there. "The only way you make somebody feel this video is for me is by being extremely specific."

**The two-sentence pattern** (Oct 2023, 574k), derived by writing out the first two sentences of
15 viral videos. Sentence one is an extreme result or problem the video will fix, addressed to
"you", carrying an extremity word ("exactly", "in one video"). **Sentence two destroys the
objection**: you say the viewer's own doubt back to them before they can act on it.

**The three boxes** (Feb 2023, 113k). Fast, so no "hey guys welcome back". **Clear**, meaning the
viewer can answer "what do I get" inside three seconds. Shocking or unique against the feed.

**The four Lego pieces** (Jul 2026, 46k). The hook creates an emotion; a visual proof that it is
not clickbait; a specific example, story or number; **a rehook that hints at a payoff at the end.**

**Three forms of specificity** (May 2026, 94k), and this is the most portable thing he says.
Niche keywords over category words, because "300 view jail" beats "content creation advice".
Numbers, because they let the listener picture the claim. Named people and faces, to borrow
attention that already exists.

**The three levels of mastery** (Feb 2026, 7.8k). Beginners steal a hook word for word and change
one or two keywords. Then **concision**: after coaching thousands, the commonest fault is taking
20 seconds to reach the point when it should be three to five. Then **tonality**: push energy
reads as desperation across the screen.

**The ideal viewer persona** (Jul 2026, 57k; Feb 2026, 13.6k). Step one is not the hook and not
the idea, it is a document naming who the viewer is, their pains and their desires. His is a
version of his younger self at college. **Every idea is a problem that persona has, stated in
their own words in the hook.** Build that document from recorded sales calls rather than from
imagination, and quote it.

**Where the hook comes from** (May 2026, 30k; Aug 2026, 28k). Search YouTube, not the feed,
because the highest-effort concepts are built there first by teams. **A short's hook is a YouTube
title and thumbnail**: the spoken line is the title, the on-screen text is the thumbnail text, the
first frame is the thumbnail image. And "scrolling like an artist": train the feed with "not
interested", then **read only the first one or two sentences of a competitor's video and stop**,
because watching further makes you a regurgitated version of them.

## His hook document, verbatim

He screen-shares his "Viral Hooks" Google Doc on camera in the Oct 2023 video. Read off the frames
with Vision OCR. His own two-sentence structure, in his own words, transcription errors his.

**SENTENCE 1: HOOK**

*Content creation*

1. Here's how I add captions to my TikToks so they go viral every single time. I use a formula I like to call SLC. Now what the hell does that mean?
2. Here's the ONLY video you will ever need to watch to understand TikTok's algorithm.
3. I regret improving myself.
4. I scripted out every single one of the hooks to my TikTok videos on this TikTok account for a month using the same exact hook structure, and in that month, I went from 0 to 100,000 followers.
5. Here's the strategy I used to go viral over and over and over again every single video. I used a strategy called the 2-second rule.
6. Here are the 3 levels of being an entrepreneur.

*Self-improvement*

1. Here's how to stop waking up in the morning, looking in the mirror, and hating what you see. Stop using your phone for the first hour of the day.
2. Here's how to get out of a slump. Maybe you're a little demotivated and you got a lot of shit to do but you just can't seem to do it and you keep procrastinating.
3. Here's how to break your shitty habits.
4. Here's how to actually focus and get your work done 5 TIMES as quickly.
5. I'm gonna show you exactly how to get unstuck in life in one video.
6. I'm gonna teach you EXACTLY how to build attraction with literally anybody in a very very specific way.
7. Here's the ABSOLUTE key to destroying the inner nice guy inside of you FOREVER.

*Lifestyle*

1. Here's a day in the life of a 22 year-old entrepreneur making $100k per month with his agency.

*Hot takes*

1. This is exactly why days don't even exist, and if you can get rid of days altogether you're gonna be a lot more productive in your life.

**SENTENCE 2: OBLITERATE OBJECTIONS**

1. Since this is TikTok, your attention span is probably dogshit, so try to make it through the rest of this video because I guarantee it will change your life.
2. What the HELL does that mean?
3. No, I'm not talking about {insert CLICHE, OVERUSED answers}.
4. Now I know I sound corny/stupid/cliche but just hear me out.
5. Now I know what you're probably thinking: ***
6. Now I know you don't wanna hear this information because you're probably thinking, ***
7. You're REALLY gonna watch this one because it's fucking crazy.
8. And this is so, SO important and I use this every day.
9. Super important if you wanna make friends, or get a girlfriend.
10. And you're not gonna wanna miss this video because it's SO good.
11. So you will never struggle with small talk ever again if you watch this video.
12. And nobody's told you this before so watch til the end because I think you might like it.
13. And they don't teach you this in business school.
14. If you want to [outcome], just implement this ONE little tactic.

**Shapes 2 and 3 are the two worth having.** "What the hell does that mean?" is the speaker asking
the viewer's question out loud, which is the cheapest way to keep a named method from reading as
jargon. "No, I'm not talking about {the cliché}" pre-empts the answer the viewer has already
dismissed, and it is the only one on the list that makes the video *more* honest rather than less.
The rest of sentence two is a promise about the video's own value, which is a claim, and on this
channel a claim has to be true.

## The second surface: what he burns into the picture

The caption track carries the spoken hook. It does not carry the **text hook**, the words burned
into the frame, which exist only as pixels. `tiktok-text-hooks.ts` reads them by OCRing a frame at
0.4s and one at 3.0s and keeping the lines present in both: captions advance every word or two and
survive one frame, a static title survives both.

**Exactly half do not have one.** Of his top 60 by era-adjusted breakout, 30 carry a static title
over and above the captions and 30 run captions only. So the text hook is not a requirement even
for him, which is worth knowing before treating it as one.

Where it exists it is a different sentence from anything he says out loud:

- "the lonely chapter when chasing your dreams"
- "STOP POSTING CONTENT NOW"
- "LEAKING my $200k/month content system (takes me 1hr a week lol)"
- "this mindset hack got me 100M views"
- "life-changing advice from a $400M founder"
- "understanding 'superhooks' got me 1.8M followers"
- "steal my hook strategy to get ur next 1M view video"
- "how to make IRRESISTIBLE hooks in just 69 seconds"
- "how to 3x your views in 76 seconds (NOT CLICKBAIT)"
- "how to NEVER run out of viral video ideas"
- "'Moving in silence' will NOT make you successful"
- "'FACELESS' CONTENT WILL NEVER MAKE YOU RICH."
- "Ludwig makes $1 MILLION a MONTH??"
- "The marketing genius behind Iman Gadzhi's success"
- "how to go viral but we both have brain damage"

**The pattern in the pair is the useful part, not the lines.** The title carries the promise and
the number; the spoken line walks in from the side and never restates it. That is the
three-surfaces rule in `SKILL.md` executed rather than described, and it is worth reading the pairs
in the corpus for that alone.

**Two limits on this section, and neither is small.** It was run on the top 60 only, all of them
winners, so **it carries no band comparison**: nothing here says a text hook separates his hits
from his median, only that half his hits have one. And the method has a false positive worth
checking before quoting anything: a video that opens on a static screen recording, his analytics,
a profile page, an earnings dashboard, a reply-to-comment sticker, returns that screen's text as
though it were a title. Six of the 30 are that case and they are obvious on sight.

## What to import, and what to leave

**Import.**

- **The objection sentence.** Say the viewer's doubt out loud in sentence two, in their words. It
  is the one move here the registry did not already carry, it costs nothing, and it makes a piece
  more honest rather than less. `customer-voice` holds the doubts in verbatim form already.
- **The three forms of specificity.** Niche keyword over category word, a number, a named person.
  This is the ultra-specific rule in `SKILL.md` broken into three checkable parts.
- **Concision as a measured fault**, three to five seconds to the point rather than twenty.
- **His calibration trick**, which is the cheapest audit in the file: after filming, scroll the feed
  for five minutes, then watch your own take. It restores the viewer's tolerance to the level the
  video will actually meet. Sits beside the eyes-closed pacing test in `video-script`.
- **Read the first two sentences of a competitor and stop.** Study the packaging without consuming
  the idea. It sharpens the remix rule in `idea-mining`, which currently says which bricks to hold
  constant but not how to look without being absorbed.

**Leave.**

- **Every promise about the video's own value.** "It will change your life", "you're not gonna
  wanna miss this", "nobody's told you this before". Unfalsifiable, and the truth rule bans them.
- **The intensifier layer**, whole. `voice-dna` and the slop list in
  `packages/lint/data/slop-words.js` reject it on sight.
- **The hooks themselves.** They are house style, per the measurement above. A shape lifted from
  the document arrives with none of the reason his videos worked and all of the reason ours would
  read as somebody else's.
- **His volume advice.** Same reason `shorts-structures.md` rejects Kallaway's: cadence is a floor,
  not a strategy, and our own control found a channel publishing weekly without a miss while its
  rolling median fell 3x.

## Where he corroborates something we already measured

Independent arrival at a rule already in the registry is worth more than a new claim, so these are
recorded as corroboration rather than as new doctrine.

| He says | Already ours |
| --- | --- |
| "Stop obsessing over hooks and retention-hacking, obsess over value per second" (Oct 2025). Months of heavy hook effort lost him followers; months at 20 minutes a video gained 100k | The subject is the reach lever and the hook is the retention lever, `idea-mining`. His own bands say the same thing louder than he does |
| Always "you", never rambling about yourself | "On camera, every I is a you", `humanizer` |
| Spend the scripting time on the hook, bullet the rest; over-scripting kills the take | "Every script ships as bullets", with the open verbatim, `video-script` |
| Confirm the promise in sentence two, seconds 5 to 10, which he attributes to Paddy Galloway | "Confirm the click in the first five seconds", `SKILL.md` |
| The spoken line is the title, the on-screen text is the thumbnail text, the frame is the thumbnail | "Three surfaces, and the failure is that they disagree", `SKILL.md` |
| State the viewer's problem in their own words | `customer-voice`, measured from 367 calls rather than imagined from a persona |
| Treat any video at 2x your average as the signal to pattern-match | Breakout against the channel's own median, `idea-mining` |

His self-correction is the important row. He sells the hook course, and in Oct 2025 he told his
audience the hooks were not the thing. The 475 openings behind this file agree with him.
