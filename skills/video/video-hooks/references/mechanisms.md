# The five mechanisms

Every hook runs on one of these. Name it before writing it, because an unnamed hook usually turns out to be two mechanisms fighting.

| Mechanism | How it works | Best for | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shock or contradiction** | Cognitive dissonance. A claim that conflicts with what the viewer believes. | Myth-busting, contrarian takes | The claim is not actually surprising, or cannot be defended |
| **Problem agitation** | Names a pain the viewer already has, then presses on it. | Known, felt problems | The audience has not felt the pain; it reads as an ad |
| **Story open** | Narrative transportation. Drops into the middle of a scene. | Personal proof, case studies | It starts with setup. In medias res or not at all |
| **Curiosity gap** | Information gap theory. Reveals the shape of what the viewer does not know, and signals that the answer is worth having. | Counterintuitive results | Only the gap is built. A withheld answer with no payoff signal reads as bait |
| **Social proof** | Authority bias. Leads with a specific result, number, or credential. | When a real number exists | The number is vague, rounded past recognition, or unverifiable |

**The curiosity gap is two conditions, not one**, and it is the row this skill writes half-finished
most often. The gap makes the viewer aware something is missing; a reason to think the answer is
useful to *them* is what makes them want it. Test the line against "is this worth knowing", not
against "does this make you want to know". The seven open shapes that carry both halves are in
[`gap-shapes.md`](gap-shapes.md).

For a founder posting their own numbers, **social proof and story open carry the most weight and are the hardest to fake**, which is exactly why they work.
