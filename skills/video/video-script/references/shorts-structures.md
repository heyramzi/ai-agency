# Short Structures

How a whole Short is built, not just its first line. The hook rules live in
`~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/video-hooks/SKILL.md`; this file covers everything between the hook and the CTA.

## The skeleton every one of his 30 posts uses

One structure, thirty times.

1. **Outcome hook, one line.** "This is how you [outcome]" / "Here's how to [outcome] in
   [compressed time]." No setup sentence before it.
2. **Name the method, one line.** "This is called the 532 Method." Every piece of advice is
   packaged as a named, countable artefact: 532 Method, Dopamine Addiction Loop, Triple Hook
   Method, 6 Hook Power Words, Internet Echo, Super Share, Flashbang Brain, Mid Flip Rehook,
   Anticipation Loading. The name makes the advice feel proprietary, gives the recap something to
   point at, and gives the artefact CTA something to deliver. Not a factual claim, so free under
   the truth rule.
2b. **The objection sentence, optional and cheap.** Between naming the method and the first step,
   say the doubt the viewer already holds, in their words. "You have heard this one before." "This
   is the part that sounds like it needs a bigger team than you have." The only move in
   [`hook-teardown.md`](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/video-hooks/references/hook-teardown.md) the registry did not already carry; it earns its place by
   conceding something true rather than promising something unfalsifiable. `conversion` holds
   the doubts verbatim, so the sentence is quoted, not invented. **Avoid the version that promises
   the video's own value** ("you are really gonna want this one"): an unfalsifiable claim the truth
   rule bans.
3. **Numbered steps, three or four.** Each step is one concept plus one concrete carrier: a
   worked example, an analogy, or a named person doing it. Never a bare definition.
4. **Rehook near the middle.** See Mid Flip below.
5. **Recap, one line, naming the parts.** "Stakes, the big question, the head fake, and the
   re-hook is all you need."
6. **Reply-trigger CTA with a named artefact.** "Comment 'formats' and I'll send you the visual
   database of all 22 formats." Every post ends here, the artefact always named, sometimes with
   social proof attached. Matches the Learned Pattern of 2026-08-01 in `video-hooks`: the
   reply-trigger CTA beats the community pointer, and the artefact must exist before publish.

Word budget observed on the transcripts: 240-435 words spoken, roughly 90-150 seconds. For a
sub-60-second Short, hold the same skeleton and cut steps, not carriers: two steps with examples
beat four steps of definitions.

## His hook anatomy, mapped to ours

His "6 Hook Power Words" (2.2k-like breakdown, transcript `DbQgNloOigA`) decompose a hook line
into: **Subject**, always "you" (`humanizer`, "On camera, every I is a you"); **Action**, the verb
that produced the outcome; **Objective**, the end result; **Contrast**, end state against base
state, which he calls the most important word in the stack and is the same mechanism as our
shock/contradiction and curiosity-gap rows; and two further optional words he withholds behind a
CTA (his own anticipation loading, working on us).

His "Triple Hook Method" (carousel `DbEF9IOjgWI`) sequences the first two sentences: **Context**
(immediate, in audio, text and visual at once), **Lean** (curiosity in one direction), then
**Contrarian Snapback** (jerk the line the other way). Snapback is a negation pivot by another
name, so our one-per-script cap still applies; the useful part is context-first sequencing, which
our banned-hook-shapes list already enforces from the other direction.

**The hook is three surfaces, and the spoken one ranks last.** His welcome email states the
ordering as a general rule: Text Hook, then Visual Hook, then Spoken Hook. The yap carousel
(`DbHC4g4GIsb`) puts a number on it: "99/100 people read the text hook first and decide whether to
stay." So a Short has three hooks to write, not one: the on-screen title text, the first frame's
visual (the proof artefact where one exists), and the spoken line. A strong spoken hook under a
weak title text still loses the read-first majority.

## Retention structures for the body

Four structures, all variations on one idea: **open a loop, pay it off, open the next one before
attention runs out.**

- **Modern Story Arc** (`DbBhseCju4D`): drop in at 70/100, spike to 90 inside the first seconds,
  then peak-release-peak every 10-15 seconds. The traditional rising-action arc loses the viewer
  before it rises.
- **Mid Flip Rehook** (`DbdRo3nuQyn`): attention span of a short-form viewer at ~25 seconds.
  Structure the piece as part one "what it is", part two "how to do it", flip between them at ~25s
  on a contrast word. The flip opens a new curiosity loop and resets the clock once.
- **Dopamine Addiction Loop** (`DaiCxAkOpNb`, his #3 post at 10k likes): Stakes (character,
  something at risk, urgency) then Big Question then Headfake (reveal B where the viewer assumed
  A) then Rehook. Great stories cascade several loops; good ones have one. Taught at length since;
  the tactics for each beat live in `storytelling`,
  [`references/addiction-loop.md`](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/content/writing/storytelling/references/addiction-loop.md):
  the stakes triad, the big question specific enough to guess at, the two-step head fake, and the
  re-hook as a timing rule. Read it before writing a Short whose body is a story rather than steps.
- **Delayed Payoff**: the Mid Flip in list shape. A counted list pays a little of the gap at every
  item, so the last one has nothing left open. Demote what has already been delivered just before
  the final item: "none of these matter unless you also do the last one." **The demotion has to be
  true**, an ordering rule rather than a transition: rank items by what actually carries the
  result, put that one last. Where items are genuinely equal, drop the move rather than invent a
  winner. Second source, also unmeasured: TikTok @lacedmedia, 12 Sep 2026, 10.1k views, 453 likes.
- **Anticipation Loading** (`DbqLTOdONbR`): dopamine peaks just before a reveal, so build tension
  on top of the open question without revealing, then reveal and immediately open the next loop.
  Two to three loops per video.

**Small Effort, Big Reward** (`DbjxM1IjmOt`), for educational pieces: frame the solution as tiny
and the payoff as large. Viewers bail the moment the solution smells like work. The effort claim
must still be true: shrink the ask by choosing genuinely small solutions, not by lying about size.

## The Lego Brick decomposition

From his welcome email: every short-form video is a stack of **11 interchangeable components** in
5 categories, developed in this order.

| Category | Bricks | His gloss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | Format | The canvas: breakdown, scenario, hero's journey, ranking… |
| Idea | Topic, Seed, Substance | The subject: topic, the one-line premise, and the facts/takes/examples that drive emotion |
| Hook | Text Hook, Visual Hook, Spoken Hook | The first exposure, in that order of importance |
| Script | Story Structure, CTA | The paint: structure, CTA, retention principles |
| Edit | Visual Layout, Elements | The brush: style, layout, pacing, captions, signature elements |

**He restates the same decomposition as seven categories** on the Open Residency podcast, 29 Jan
2026: Topic, Angle (`idea-mining` calls this the seed), Hook structure (the three surfaces, per
`SKILL.md`), Story structure (case study, breakdown, listicle, narrative), Visual format (split
screen, full screen, inset circle, POV), Key visuals (A-roll, B-roll, graphics, proof artefact),
Audio. It is the more usable version because every category is something a person can be handed.

**The remix rule in numbers: hold six, change one.** Topic and angle change every time anyway; of
the five craft bricks, borrow the ones already proven in the niche and invent on exactly one.
Inventing on several at once is why a beginner's first fifty pieces teach nothing: too much moved
to attribute anything. Two uses: **diagnosis vocabulary** (name the brick before proposing a fix,
"the hook is fine, the substance is thin" beats "it didn't work") and **the remix rule** itself
(find a brick stack already working, keep ~8 bricks, swap ~3; the skeleton and retention
structures here are the inherited bricks, topic, substance and proof are the swap, and the truth
rule bites on the substance bricks).

**One per-video habit worth adopting: the improvement hypothesis.** His condition #2: every video
tests one named change, stated before publishing, so the result is attributable. This slots into
the skill's execution loop as "what is this Short's experiment?".

**A caution on his volume advice.** Conditions #1 and #4 (3-7 videos/week, skills lock in around
rep 50) are coaching heuristics. Our own control-tested series found the counterexample: Ross
Harkness published every Sunday without a miss while his rolling median fell 3x, **cadence is a
floor, not a strategy** (`competitor-evidence.md`). Volume buys learning reps only if condition #2
rides along.

## Template for a Short in your own register

The same skeleton, calm and evidenced, with no hype intensifiers. Every `[number]` must come from
your proof table or a screen you can show.

```
HOOK      This is how [outcome the viewer wants], in [true compressed time].
NAME      It is called the [Named Method]. [count] steps.
STEP 1    [Concept]. [Worked example in the viewer's world, not yours.]
STEP 2    [Concept]. [Example or analogy.]
~25s FLIP [What part one fixes, as a fact.] [The specific thing that breaks in part two.]
STEP 3    [Concept]. [Example, ideally your own measured number, shown on screen.]
RECAP     [Part], [part], [part]. That's the whole method.
CTA       Comment "[trigger word]" and I'll send you [named artefact that exists].
```

The name must be earned (a real method you use, countable), the flip happens once, and the
artefact is built before the Short is published.

**On a list-shaped Short the FLIP line is the demotion**, so it reads `[Step 1] and [step 2] do
nothing on their own. The one that moves [the number] is the last.` Choosing an order that makes
the sentence true is the work.

**The flip carries a fact across the boundary, never an announcement.** The old version of that
line read "but knowing part one is not the point, here's how you actually do it", a negation pivot
on top of a throat-clearing opener: delete the transition and nothing stands. Name what breaks in
part two and the re-hook does its own work. [`story-locks.md`](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/video-hooks/references/story-locks.md), lock 5, holds the
test and the house-legal bank.
