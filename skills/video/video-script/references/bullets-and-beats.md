# Bullets and the beat budget

How a script is written down, and how its length is budgeted. The doctrine that decides
what goes in it is in `SKILL.md`.

## Every script ships as bullets

**A script is bullet points, never prose paragraphs.** Read from bullets and it comes
out natural; prose makes the speaker recite, and the recitation is audible in the take.

**A bullet is a prompt spoken around, not a sentence read out.** Under 14 words, and
the words are the thing itself: the click, the field name on screen, the number, the
point being made. Writing bullets was never the hard part; keeping them short is. One
generated script cleared every other rule on this page and was still unrecordable,
because every bullet was a 30 to 50 word sentence with a dash in front of it. The
verdict from the person holding the camera: "I am literally not understanding what I
need to record."

| | A bullet | A sentence with a dash |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | `- Settings, App Center, MCP Servers. The panel is new.` | `- This one does not get finished on camera, and the reason is the point rather than a skipped step.` |

- One bullet per beat, in the order the beat is spoken. Two ideas joined by "and" or
  "because" are two bullets.
- Nest sub-bullets for the parts of a beat, the on-screen note, or an open question.
- **No stage directions.** A bullet is a thing that gets said or done on camera, never
  an instruction about how to say it. "Land it:", "Say it plainly:", "Name the artefact:",
  "The turn:" and every cue of that family are cut. They read as a director talking to
  the talent, and the talent is the author.
- **Three things are written out in full and wrapped in quotes**, and they are the only
  lines allowed past the word cap: the opening lines of the cold open, where the wording
  is load-bearing and a paraphrase loses the hook; the closing ask; and any measured
  number, which is spoken exactly and never rounded. The quotes are the marker, so no cue
  word is needed and the teleprompter styles them from the quotes alone. Everything
  between the intro and the outro is a prompt, with no exceptions but the numbers.
- Editorial notes ("On screen", "Why it is built this way", cut instructions) stay prose
  and sit outside the bullet list, because nobody says them out loud.

## The beat budget

**The body is budgeted in beats, and a beat is one bullet.** A word budget cannot be
spent any way except by lengthening bullets, which is exactly how one generated script
turned into prose: the expansion pass was told to hit a word count, and it hit it by
growing the bullets it already had rather than finding the steps it had skipped. Given
a beat target the only way to hit it is to add beats.

**One beat runs about seven seconds on camera.** Measured on a script sized to 195 wpm
before anybody looked at its bullets: 3,543 words over a planned 20:44, carried by 179
bullets, which is 6.95 seconds a bullet. The how-to rewrite of the same video lands in
the same place at 169 beats. So the winning nineteen minutes is roughly **158 to 185
beats**, which is the same evidence as the 3,600 to 4,200 words, converted.

- Short of the target means the block is **missing beats**, never missing words. Find the
  step it skipped: the exact click, the field name on screen, the number the viewer sees,
  the mistake most people make here and what it costs.
- The word count is still recorded, because it is what the three-run control measured. It
  is a reading of the script, not a target to hit.
- The teleprompter paces in **beats per minute**, not words per minute, for the same
  reason: the body is spoken around rather than read, so its word count says how much was
  typed and not how long the take runs.
