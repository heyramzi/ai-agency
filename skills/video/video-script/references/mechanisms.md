# The five mechanisms

Every hook runs on one of these. Name it before writing it, because an unnamed hook usually turns out to be two mechanisms fighting.

| Mechanism | How it works | Best for | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shock or contradiction** | Cognitive dissonance. A claim that conflicts with what the viewer believes. | Myth-busting, contrarian takes | The claim is not actually surprising, or cannot be defended |
| **Problem agitation** | Names a pain the viewer already has, then presses on it. | Known, felt problems | The audience has not felt the pain; it reads as an ad |
| **Story open** | Narrative transportation. Drops into the middle of a scene. | Personal proof, case studies | It starts with setup. In medias res or not at all |
| **Curiosity gap** | Information gap theory. Reveals the shape of what the viewer does not know, and signals that the answer is worth having. | Counterintuitive results | Only the gap is built. A withheld answer with no payoff signal reads as bait |
| **Social proof** | Authority bias. Leads with a specific result, number, or credential. | When a real number exists | The number is vague, rounded past recognition, or unverifiable |

For a founder posting their own numbers, **social proof and story open carry the most weight and are the hardest to fake**, which is exactly why they work.

## The curiosity gap, in depth

The curiosity gap is the mechanism most often written half-finished, and it is two conditions, not
one. A gap on its own only makes the viewer aware something is missing; it does not make them want
it. **A hook has to open a gap the viewer's brain wants to close, and give that brain a reason to
believe the answer will be useful to them.** Both halves, in the same sentence. Test the line
against "is this worth knowing", not against "does this make you want to know".

This is what separates a hook from bait. Bait passes the first test and fails the second: the
viewer can feel the answer is being withheld, but nothing in the line suggests the answer changes
anything for them, so the gap closes itself by being ignored.

It also explains why the ultra-specific rule in `SKILL.md` works. Specificity is how the payoff
signal gets carried: a situation with a number, a tool or a person in it tells the viewer the
answer is about their week rather than about the subject in general. A general claim opens a
general gap, which is a gap nobody is short of.

### The third condition: the gap has to be guessable

Added 29 Aug 2026. The two conditions above are about wanting the answer and valuing it. A third
one decides whether the payoff can land at all: **the viewer has to be able to guess wrong.**

A gap is opened by withholding, and a payoff is paid by breaking a prediction. If the line gives
nothing to predict with, no prediction forms, and the reveal has nothing to contrast against, so it
arrives as information rather than as a surprise. This is the mechanism `storytelling`,
[`addiction-loop.md`](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/content/writing/storytelling/references/addiction-loop.md) calls
prediction error, and it is why an empty teaser fails even when it is genuinely withholding
something good.

| Not guessable | Guessable |
| --- | --- |
| Something happened on that call that changed everything | She said one thing in the first 30 minutes that killed the retainer |
| You are not going to believe what we found in the account | The account had 4 automations firing on the same trigger |
| Everything changed after that | The fix took 20 minutes and it broke the invoicing for a week |

The right-hand column names a situation, a number or a person, the same ultra-specific rule
`SKILL.md` states from the identification side: it lets the viewer recognise themselves, and it
gives their brain the material to make a guess with.

**The limit on the other side is overload.** Enough to guess, not so much that the answer is
already obvious, at which point the gap closes itself before the line ends. That is the same
failure as the hook answering itself, arriving through the setup instead of through the verdict.

### Scenario injection: where the specific situation comes from

Added 14 Sep 2026. The rule above says the line needs a situation in it. It does not say where to
find one, and the default is to reach into the topic, which is the one place it is not.

**The topic is what the video teaches. The situation is the hour the viewer already lost by not
knowing it.** They are rarely the same words. "Three clauses every landlord needs in their lease"
identifies nobody, because nobody thinks about their own week in clauses. "If you are a landlord,
you should not be unblocking a toilet at 11pm" is the same video, opening on a night the viewer
either had or did not have. The second line is about a consequence of the topic, one step
downstream, where the cost is actually felt.

So the generation step is: fix the topic, list the moments it costs somebody, and take the one
carrying a clock and a physical object. A clock makes it guessable in the sense above, and an object
makes it sensory, which a general statement of the same pain cannot carry.

**Two constraints.** The moment must not appear in the payoff, or the opening has summarised the
video instead of opening it. And the moment has to be one the audience really has: an invented
scenario is invented material, which the one rule in
`~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/video-hooks/SKILL.md` bans as firmly as an
invented number. `conversion` holds moments said out loud on 367 calls, so the injection is
harvested before it is written.

The source names the move scenario injection. A name is free under the truth rule, and the
conditional it fills was already the shape in
`~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/video-hooks/SKILL.md`; what is new is looking one
step past the subject. The list-shaped body it feeds is Delayed Payoff in
`shorts-script`'s [`shorts-structures.md`](~/Studio/vibe-kit/ai-doc/skills/video/scripting/shorts-script/references/shorts-structures.md). Single source, no control test: TikTok
@lacedmedia, 12 Sep 2026, 10.1k views on 453 likes, titled "How to increase the Watch Time on your
videos".

## Seven open shapes that carry both halves

Sentence frames, not lines. Each one opens the gap in the last clause and signals the payoff in
the first, which is why they hold across subjects. They are starting shapes for the variant pass
in `SKILL.md` step 4, and every one of them still has to be filled with something measured.

| Shape | Where the payoff signal sits |
| --- | --- |
| If I had to start from zero, here is exactly what I would do first | The viewer is at or near zero, and "first" ranks the answer for them |
| Nobody talks about... | Scarcity of the information itself, so hearing it is an advantage |
| I tested [method] for [duration], here is what happened | Somebody already paid the cost of finding out |
| I stopped [action], here is what has happened since | A removal the viewer could also make, with the result attached |
| I do not think we talk enough about the fact that... | A named blind spot, so the answer corrects something they hold |
| I wish I had known this sooner | Regret prices the answer: late cost the speaker something |
| This might surprise you, but... | Flags the answer as contradicting what they hold, so it updates them |

Shapes 3 and 4 are the two that carry a real experiment, so they are the two the truth rule binds
hardest and the two worth reaching for first on this channel. Shapes 2, 5 and 7 make a claim about
what the field discusses or what the viewer believes, which is a claim, and it has to be true.
Shape 6 is the weakest of the seven here: it is autobiography, and `learned-patterns.md` already
records a hook rejected on exactly that ground.

**Every one of them ends unfinished.** None delivers its verdict in the same breath, which is the
"the hook must not answer itself" rule in `SKILL.md` showing up as a sentence shape rather than as
a warning. Four of the seven are literally open-ended, and the creator reads them aloud with the
blank left unspoken, which is the cleanest demonstration of that rule available.

**They are frames, not lines, and that is the limit of what they are worth.** A frame carries the
gap-plus-payoff structure and nothing else. The ultra-specific rule in `SKILL.md` still has to be
satisfied by whatever gets poured in, so "I tested [method] for [duration]" is not a hook until the
method and the duration are real and named.

## The eighth shape: the comparison the viewer already makes

Added 10 Sep 2026. The seven above are first-person or field-scoped: the speaker went and found
out. There is one more shape, and it is second-person and accusatory. It names a comparison the
viewer already runs in private, then withholds the reason.

> Why are people dumber than you making more money than you?

Both halves land in one sentence. The gap is the reason, and the payoff signal is that the answer
concerns a specific person the viewer already resents, so it is about their week by construction.
It is also guessable in the sense above: the viewer forms an answer before reading, usually "luck",
which is the prediction the piece then breaks.

**The failure condition is flattery.** The shape only works while the comparison is one the viewer
holds and the line does not resolve in their favour. Rewrite it as "why smart people stay broke"
and the accusation becomes a compliment, the viewer is no longer implicated, and it drops back to
shape 5. The other risk is that the payoff must be a mechanism: a shape this sharp opening onto
"work hard and stay consistent" is the bait case, which is what the source below actually paid.

Single source: an X article by @notdwd, 7 Sep 2026, 2,398 words. A 467-follower account took
14,864 views, 32x its following, on 285 bookmarks against 233 likes. Bookmarks over likes reads as
save bait in `whats-the-moat`, and the body is a third-hand summary of a YouTube video summarising
an MIT article. The title carried it, so the title is the only part measured here.

## Where the counted promise actually landed

The reel is titled as seven hooks and puts five on screen. The other two are in the post caption,
where the creator says she forgot them in the edit. So the promise was paid, just not in the
medium that made it, and a viewer who never opens the caption counts five. `two-windows.md` calls a
counted promise a contract; this is the cheap version of breaking it, an edit error rather than a
withholding, and it still costs the same on delivery. The separate comment-for-more ask is for 30
further hooks, not for the missing two.

Single source, no control test: Instagram reel `Db6C7oppvwe`, @growithfanny, 11 Aug 2026, in
French. It sits below anything measured on this channel. The gap-plus-payoff test is the part worth
keeping; the shapes are one creator's list, not a measured set.
