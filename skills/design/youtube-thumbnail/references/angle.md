# The angle

Step 1b of the workflow, and the one the skill did not have until 25 Aug 2026. It runs
before the variant is picked and before a word of the script exists.

An angle is three things, written in this order:

1. **The claim.** The one sentence a viewer believes the second the frame is read,
   written the way they would say it. Not a description of the picture.
2. **The payoff.** The beat of the video that proves the claim, quoted rather than
   summarised.
3. **The title.** The title that claim wants beside the frame.

Everything the rest of this skill teaches is how a claim gets drawn. This page is about
whether the claim is worth drawing.

## The packaging is one unit, and half of it was never held to the law

[`copy.md`](copy.md) bans a category name, a sentiment and a restatement on the frame,
because each is a measured control marker. Step 1 then took the title as a given: "the
exact words YouTube will show". So the law ran on four words and was waved through on
the sixty characters sitting next to them.

The ops-ceiling video is the case. Title: **Why Your Agency Hits the Ops Ceiling**. It
names a category, it asks the topic as a question, and it makes no claim. The frame then
spent its four words repairing it: `HIRING WON'T FIX IT`, `SALES ISN'T THE PROBLEM`,
`BEST MONTH. WORST WEEK.` — three real claims, each carrying the video, none of them in
the title.

**Contest the title.** Return the title the angle wants, or the existing title verbatim
where it already carries the claim. A title arriving from keyword research has been
optimised for a search term and never for a click, and those are different jobs.

## A claim with no payoff is a hole in the video, not a bad thumbnail

This stage runs **before** the script. That is the whole reason the angle belongs here
rather than at render time.

A claim the video cannot pay off has two honest endings, and picking between them is
the point of writing the payoff down:

- the claim is bait and gets cut, or
- the video gains the beat that pays it.

Found at this stage, the second one costs a bullet in a script that has not been written
yet. Found after the shoot, it costs a pickup or a lie. The frame is the cheapest place
in the pipeline to discover that the video does not actually say the strongest thing it
could say.

Write the payoff as a quote. A summary of a beat cannot be checked; a line can.

## Three claims, not three layouts

The three concepts are `safe read`, `owned read`, `break`. Those name three **design**
strategies, and nothing stopped all three arguing the same beat three ways. A set like
that is one concept rendered three times, and the choice on screen is a choice of
furniture.

**The three concepts make three different claims about three different beats.** That is
what makes the thumbnails stage a decision about the video instead of a decision about
a picture.

## Lift the claim out of what he actually says

The brief is a production document. A claim written from it is a claim about a plan.

A line already said on camera is a promise that is already paid, and it arrives in his
voice for free. The ops-ceiling take at 00:18:

> "Your best month, and you know it already if you reach that, is the one where your
> delivery breaks."

Bounded, counter-intuitive, his, and stronger than anything writable from the brief.
`BEST MONTH. WORST WEEK.` is that line compressed to four words.

So: where a script or a transcript exists, quote from it. The generator is handed the
script's beats when the concept has one, and a redraw after scripting is the run where
the claims stop being written about a plan.

## No scan, no claim about the field

A set drawn with no niche scan has seen no competing frame and no competing title. The
20 Aug ops-ceiling set was drawn that way and its bets still read "nobody in this SERP
says that" and "no competitor has drawn the ceiling as an object". Both were invented,
both looked like evidence, and one of them was the reason a concept got picked.

Without a scan, the bet is written about **this** frame only.

## Where the angle goes after it is chosen

The pick is recorded, not remembered. On `/youtube/concepts` the thumbnails stage carries
a **Run with this angle** control per frame; it writes `chosen` onto the stored concept
set, and the script generator reads it through `chosenAngle` and `packagingBrief`
(the concept schema).

The script is then told the claim is a debt: paid in the first block, in the words the
frame uses, never left to the proof block. Unrecorded, the script was written from the
brief alone and the frame's promise reached the edit as a surprise. The ops-ceiling take
is what that looks like: three frames claiming hiring will not fix it, and a video that
opens on a course plug and a credential and does not reach the claim until 01:33.

## What kills an angle

- It restates the title. Two sentences reading the same idea, and one of them was free.
- It names a category. `CEO Dashboard` is a shelf, not a reason.
- It cannot be quoted against the video, and nobody adds the beat.
- It asserts something about competitors the run never saw.
- It is the same claim as the frame beside it, in a different composition.
