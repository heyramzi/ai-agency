# The YouTube video board

Read before laying out any board a YouTube video talks over. It's what the evidence says a board
has to carry, and the eval at the bottom is the pass every board clears before the author records.

## The evidence it rests on

The ClickUp GTD Template video (Mar 2024, Whimsical board `GTD Principles`) is the channel's most
engaging: 10,456 views, with 202 likes and 44 comments, more than any other upload on either
channel. 3 measured facts made it work, and they are the rules below:

- The why ran 6:57 of 18:08 (38%) before ClickUp opened: a before/after, the 5 steps, a
  weekly review, the Horizons of Focus pyramid, then a decision flowchart.
- The flowchart held people. Retention dips to 23% at the 15 to 20% mark, then climbs back to
  31% and holds from 4:32 to 6:20, which is the flowchart on screen. Commenters asked "from which
  website you are creating this flow chart" and "can you share the presentation/flowchart".
- Half the board was pictures. 5 of its 9 top-level items were pasted images (a Drake
  before/after meme, the book cover, the steps card, the review table, the pyramid).

The counter-example: "Why Your Agency Hits a Delivery Ceiling" (Sep 2026) spent 46% on the why
and got 107 views. It had slides, stock B-roll and a testimonial wall, and no single mechanism a
viewer could take away. Time on the why isn't the lever. A mechanism worth saving is.

An outside board video agrees: Jar Builds Brands, "meta ads feel illegal when you do this" (Oct
2026), had 3,217 views in 2 days on 1,150 subscribers. It's a Miro board packed with real
screenshots, ads and charts. Its mechanism is a split into 2 pillars (creative and offer), it
gives 72% of its runtime to the why, and he circles things on the board in red while he talks.
His hook proves the claim in 20 seconds with a live refresh of a $31k day.

Watch hours come from somewhere else: the channel's top 3 by watch time are long beginner
tutorials (the French guide alone has 1,609 hours). So bet on a broad beginner topic with search demand,
and tell it as a board.

## The spine

One canvas with a frame per chapter, in the script's order:

1. **Before/after.** Put the viewer's pain next to the calm state they want, in their words.
   A known meme format or 2 photos beats 2 bullet lists. The proof (a real number, a result, a
   screenshot) sits in this frame, because the hook shows it inside 30 seconds.
2. **The method in steps.** Numbered, with an icon and one line per step.
3. **The deeper frame.** The structure the method sits in, such as a pyramid or a table of
   cadences. Skip it when the topic has none. Don't invent one.
4. **The decision flowchart.** The one mechanism a viewer can apply without the product:
   diamonds for yes/no questions, boxes for where things end up. This is the frame people save,
   so it gets the most room and the cleanest layout on the board.
5. **The tool map.** What each part of the method becomes in the product, then the demo starts.
   The product first appears at about 35% of the runtime.
6. **Tips or the close.** 3 to 5 items, back on the board after the demo.

The board itself is the free download ("get the flowchart"). That replaces a lead-magnet plug in
the first 2 minutes, which is where Delivery Ceiling lost people.

## The visual bar

- Pictures carry the concrete. A real object, a screenshot, a cover, a face reacting: at
  least 3 per board, placed with `wb-image`. Text alone reads as a slide deck.
- One shape per concept, no 2 neighbours alike without a reason. Before/after, steps,
  pyramid, flowchart and table are 5 different shapes because they're 5 different ideas.
- Readable at screen-share zoom. A frame gets recorded zoomed to fill 1920x1080, so a label
  that can't be read in the frame's PNG at that size gets cut or enlarged.
- Colour means something. One accent for questions (the flowchart diamonds), one for end
  states, grey for structure. A colour that only decorates gets removed.
- Mark it live. Circle and underline with tldraw's draw tool while talking. A board that only
  pans gives the eye nothing new for minutes at a time.
- Air between frames, at least a frame's own height of gap, so a pan between chapters reads
  as a new chapter.
- No text block on screen longer than 30 seconds of talking. GTD's weekly review table sat
  static from 2:45 to 3:20, and that's where attention dropped. Split it or reveal it in parts.

The analogy rules and the non-technical register belong to the `youtube` skill. Labels go through
`humanizer` like any first-party copy.

## Eval

Run it on the finished board by reading its PNGs fresh, one frame at a time. A check passes or
fails. Loop until all pass, 3 rounds at most. If a check can't pass without inventing a fact (no
real before/after exists, no decision in the topic), say so in the report and leave that frame
out; don't fill it.

1. The topic is a problem or method people search for on its own, not one product feature.
2. Frames run in the video's order, and the product's first frame sits at 30 to 40% of the
   script's runtime.
3. There's exactly one mechanism frame: a decision flowchart with at least 2 yes/no diamonds and
   3 end states, or a split into pillars that ends in actions.
4. The first frame holds a real proof, and the script shows it inside 30 seconds.
5. The board holds at least 3 images, and every one shows something real.
6. No 2 neighbouring frames use the same shape.
7. All labels read in the frame's PNG at 1920x1080.
8. No text block is meant to stay on screen longer than 30 seconds of the script.
9. No frame pitches. Testimonial walls, "who this is for" slides and prices stay off the board.
10. A colour on the board stands for one thing, and you can say what.
11. `humanizer` exits 0 on every label.
