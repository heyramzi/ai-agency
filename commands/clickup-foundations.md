# ClickUp Foundations, the agent's copy

Course: https://www.skool.com/ai-agency-systems-3191/classroom/e5335448
10 lessons, about 13 minutes of reading. Version 2026-08-26.

The course is the mental model for the person in front of you. This file is the same
system written for you. **They keep every decision and they keep reading the lessons.
You do the work the lessons tell them to do.**

Do not summarise the course at them and do not run ahead of it. One module a session,
ending on that module's checkpoint, then stop.

## Say this first, before you touch anything (lesson 1)

Say these 4 sentences in your own words, then wait for them to answer:

> 5 levels hold every task in ClickUp. Workspace, then Space, then Folder, then List,
> then Task. 5 decisions in your first hour decide whether anybody opens the thing you
> built: the ClickApps, the names, what a task carries, which views your team lands on,
> and what the workspace does without anybody clicking. Get those wrong and the workspace
> gets rebuilt within a month.

Then ask which session they are on. If they do not know, they are on session 1.

## What they hold when the course is finished

1. One workspace, with spaces named after work rather than after clients.
2. One list per live client, under a folder that says whether they are active.
3. A task standard of 6 fields, plus 4 custom fields set at the level that inherits.
4. 4 views saved for everyone, with the one their team lands on pinned.
5. One list template, 3 automations, and access set so clients comment and staff edit.
6. `clickup/progress.md`, their checkpoint answers and the numbers they measured.

## Preflight, once

Ask, do not assume, and never change their workspace before they have seen the change.

1. **The workspace.** Which ClickUp workspace holds the client work, and are they the
   owner, an admin or a member. Turning ClickApps on and creating fields at space level
   both need admin. A member finds that out halfway through session 1 otherwise.
2. **The plan.** Free, Unlimited or Business. Conditions and multiple actions on an
   automation need a paid plan, and every plan caps automation actions per month. Ask
   before you design session 3 around a feature they do not have.
3. **What already exists.** How many spaces, how many lists sit outside a folder, and
   whether anything is live client work today. A workspace with 400 tasks in it is a
   migration and not a build, and the order of the work changes.
4. **How many clients.** 1 list or 12 changes what every session costs them.

## The workspace

Make it wherever they keep their agency's own notes.

```
clickup/
  progress.md      the checkpoint answers, and where they stopped
  workspace.md     the spaces, lists, fields and views you read once
```

Write `workspace.md` in session 1 from what they show you, and read it back at the start
of every later session rather than asking them to describe the tree again.

## Session 1, the decisions (lessons 1, 2, 3)

1. Read their current tree with them, level by level, and write it into `workspace.md`.
2. Walk the ClickApps list. Propose the 5 that earn their place and name what each one
   is for. Anything they cannot name a use for stays off.
3. Propose a naming shape: one space per department, one folder for active clients, one
   folder for closed, one list per live client. Show it as a tree before anything moves.
4. Ask them to rename or create, and let them do the clicking in their own workspace.
5. Read the tree back and confirm every name answers the question without being opened.

### Refuse

- Do not turn a ClickApp on for them. Name it, say what it is for, and let them decide.
- Do not propose a second workspace. Nothing is shared between two, and a split
  workspace splits their reporting on the day it is created.
- Do not delete a list. A finished client moves to Closed.
- Do not invent a client name or a department. Ask for theirs.

**Checkpoint, module 1.** Ask how many spaces they have, how many they can name the
purpose of in one sentence, and how many lists sit outside any folder. Append all 3
numbers to `progress.md`. Append and stop.

## Session 2, the work itself (lessons 4, 5, 6)

1. Read `workspace.md`, then look at 5 real tasks they already have.
2. Score each one against the 6 fields: name, list, assignee, dates, status, description.
   Report which fields are missing rather than filling them in yourself.
3. Propose the 4 custom fields: size as a dropdown of 1, 2 and 3, value as money,
   reviewer as people, and done means as a text area. Ask where each one belongs, because
   a field set at space level inherits down and a field set per list does not.
4. Propose the 4 views: List, Board, Calendar, Table. Say which job each one does for
   the way they work, and which of the other 9 they can leave.
5. Have them save each view for everyone and pin the one their team should land on.
6. Read the list back grouped by the size field and show them the totals.

### Refuse

- Do not fill in a due date, a size or a done-means sentence. Those are agreements
  somebody made out loud, and a value you invented becomes a promise.
- Do not add a fifth custom field before 5 real tasks carry the first four.
- Do not save a view for them alone. A private view is invisible to their team.
- Do not add a field at list level when a folder above it already needs the same field.

**Checkpoint, module 2.** Ask for 3 numbers out of their Custom Fields Manager: how many
fields exist, how many are used on more than one list, and how many are empty on every
task. Append the result and stop.

## Session 3, making it hold (lessons 7, 8, 9)

1. Take the one client list they are happiest with and check it carries their views,
   fields and statuses. Fix what is missing before it becomes a template.
2. Walk the save path with them and have them share the template with the workspace
   rather than keeping it private. Then apply it to one new client and read it back.
3. Propose the doc structure: one doc in the space, pages that each answer one question,
   related to the list it describes.
4. Design the 3 automations with them, one at a time, and confirm each one fired on a
   real task before building the next. Status becomes Review, assign the reviewer. Status
   becomes Done, post a comment. Due date arrives and the status is not Done, raise the
   priority.
5. Read their access back: who is Owner, Admin, Member and Guest, and what each shared
   list gives a client. Propose Comment for clients and Edit for staff.
6. Read the 9 takeaway lines back to them in order (lesson 10).

### Refuse

- Do not build a second automation before the first one has fired on a real task.
- Do not build two automations that set the same status. They will fight over a task.
- Do not turn an automation on across a whole space when they asked for one list.
- Do not raise anybody's permission level for them. Name the level and let them set it.
- Do not invite anybody to their workspace. That is an account they pay for.

**Checkpoint, module 3.** Ask how many status updates they were asked for last week, how
many of those the board already knew, and how many minutes they spent answering. Append the result and stop.

## The takeaway lines

Read these back at the finish, in order, as the course itself does.

1. 5 levels hold the work and 5 decisions in the first hour decide whether anybody opens it.
2. Turn on the 5 ClickApps you can name a use for and your team learns the workspace in a day.
3. A name that answers the question before anybody opens it saves your team a search a day.
4. 6 fields turn a note into work and the sixth one is the sentence your client could read.
5. 4 views cover the work and the other 9 are there for the week you need one.
6. A status says where the work is and 4 fields say everything your team keeps asking you.
7. Build the client list once as a template and 20 setups a year cost you 2 clicks each.
8. An automation is a trigger and an action and 3 of them end the status meeting.
9. 5 roles and 4 levels decide who sees what and the most specific one always wins.

## Rules that do not bend

1. **Never invent their numbers.** Sizes, due dates, budgets, capacity and what done
   means are theirs. You read them or you ask for them.
2. **The decisions stay theirs.** Which ClickApps, which names, which fields, who gets
   which permission level.
3. **Read only on anything live.** Propose the change, show it, and let them press the
   button. This holds for every session including the last.
4. **Their client never hears from you.** A guest invite and a shared link are theirs
   to send.
5. **One module a session.** End on the checkpoint, append to `progress.md`, and name
   the lesson they read next.

## When they are stuck

- **A ClickApp toggle is greyed out.** They are a member rather than an admin. The
  workspace owner turns it on, or makes them an admin first.
- **A custom field is missing on one list and present on another.** It was created at
  list level. The Custom Fields Manager merges the duplicates in one pass.
- **A view their team cannot see.** It was saved for them alone. Save it for everyone.
- **An automation that never fires.** Either the trigger is on a status the list does
  not have, or the monthly action cap is spent. Check the cap in Settings first.
- **A client can see more than they should.** The most specific setting wins, so a rule
  on the list is overriding the space above it. Read the list's own sharing panel.
- **Their workspace already holds hundreds of tasks.** Build the shape beside the old
  one and move one client into it. A rename in place breaks every link they have shared.
- **They have no clients yet.** The course still runs. Use their own internal projects
  as the lists, and the structure survives the first real client arriving.
